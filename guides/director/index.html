Build the game in Godot itself

The HorrorStudio Director is a Godot plugin. The scene is the world: drag in rooms, doors and characters, and make your own models into things with parts (a kettle, a letter, a hiding place). The story is one file beside it, written in a page, the Director Manager: quests one at a time, moments, conversations, states and endings, naming the scene's things by id. Press F5: the Director watches what the player does and sets the world to match, on the studio's own runtime (the walking, the clicking, the inventory, the flashlight, hiding, the HUD, the pause menu, the saves).

1Start a project

From the studio's folder, make a new Godot project with everything in it. --sample starts you from the sample house (a hall, a kitchen, a locked bedroom, a letter, a key, a kettle, Mari, a jumpscare and two endings); leave it out for an empty game with one room.

python tools/godot_director.py new ~/Games/MyGame --sample

Open the folder in Godot 4.7 (Import in the project manager, pick its project.godot). Both plugins are already switched on. Press F5 and you're playing.

main.tscn

The world: an HSGame with the levels under it. F5 plays it.

director/

The story: director.json, the Director file (§6), and history/, the versions before each save.

addons/

The Director, the runtime (horrorstudio) and the look (horrorstudio_look). Don't edit them here: they're copies.

game/

The sound shelf (240 sounds by name), the little things' lives, and the Character Studio exports your characters wear (people/).

animations/, faces/

The animation library, empty in a new game: drop in the body clips (Kimodo's .npz, Godot animations) and the face clips it needs. They're cards on the panel's Animate tab (§5).

kimodo/, bodies/

Kimodo's stages and the motions it saved, MHR's body to try them on.

guide/

This guide, also opened by the dock's Guide button.

Already have a Godot project? Put the add-ons into it (again after every pull of the studio: they're copies):

python tools/godot_director.py install ~/Games/MyGame
Tip. install ... --link makes the add-ons links into the studio's folder instead of copies, so a pull reaches the project straight away. Both commands are also in the launcher (./studio, the Godot section).

A game you wrote on the director's desk comes in as a Director project of its own, the desk's levels as these nodes (rooms, doors, things with their models, characters, spots, areas, paths, lights) and its story as the Director file (each task a quest, its beats moments, its endings; the cutscenes and the desk's choices as the desk had them, marked ◆):

python tools/godot_director.py from-desk Pilot

It lands in Saved/Godot/Pilot_director (or a folder you name); --force makes it again over the old one, so do your Godot work on it after you're done on the desk. --play plays it through headless to check it.

2The editor, the panel and the dock

Everything in the world is a node in the scene tree: rooms, doors, things, characters, areas, spots, paths. You add them from the HorrorStudio panel along the bottom of the editor (it can float on a second screen too: its ⋮ menu). Its Build tab has everything a game is made of, as picture cards in categories (Player, Building, Lights & power, Pick up & look, Hide & rest, Kitchen, Fire & smoke, Electronics, Puzzles, Carry & play, Chores, People, Areas & paths, Scares, Reactions):

  • Click a card: it's added in front of the editor's camera, in the selected room.
  • Drag a card onto your model (in the 3D view or the Scene tree): that model becomes it (§4).
  • Select your model first, then click a card: the same, one click. Search for any card at the top.

The column on the right, This object, says what's selected in plain words (a kettle: how it starts, what the player can do, where its steam comes out; its name in the story and what in the story names it), and Make it plain again takes the thing away and leaves your model. Godot's own Add Child Node works too (search for "HS"). Select a node and the Inspector shows its settings, only the ones that matter for what it is.

What happens in the game isn't in the scene: it's the Director file (§6), written in the panel's Story tab (§8) or the Director Manager (§9), in the same words. Animate has the project's animations and faces as cards, given to a character with a click or a drag (§5). Test has Play the game, Play this scene (a level open on its own plays alone, as F6 does) and the game checked as it will play: what won't play right and what's worth a look, each line clicked to go to its node. At the top of the editor, beside 2D, 3D and Script, Story Flow shows the whole story as a flowchart (§6). The dock under the Inspector keeps the same views close to the Inspector (a row you pick in it opens right above it). Its tabs:

DirectorThe story as the Director file has it: the quests in play order with their steps and what comes next, the endings, the conversations, the moments, the states. ▶ plays, ▶ Here plays from the picked quest, Manager opens the page that writes it.
ThingsThe items, notes, things to use, characters, rooms, areas, doors, spots, paths and lights, with their ids.
CheckThe game compiled as it will play, and what's wrong.
KimodoSend a room to Kimodo, bring a motion back.

Under the tabs: a line saying what just happened (or what's missing), and the Guide button, which opens this page (so does Project → Tools → HorrorStudio Director guide).

Rooms, doors, things, characters, areas, spots and paths each have an id (the Inspector's first field): it's how the story names them. It's the node's name in lowercase with underscores (BedroomKey is bedroom_key) and follows the name when you rename the node, until you type an id of your own. Each time you save a scene the plugin writes down its ids, for the Manager's pickers.
Tip. Drag the dock's edge to make it wider; the rows read whole at about 600 pixels.

3Build a level

A level is an HSLevel: a place the game plays in. Build it under the HSGame, or as its own scene (its root an HSLevel) dropped into main.tscn. The story plays where the player is: a new level is only for a new map (the player drives somewhere else). Inside it:

Rooms

An HSRoom makes a floor, four walls and a ceiling from its size (the space inside: width, height, depth). Its origin is the middle of the floor; north is Godot's −Z. Turn walls off with walls, give it your own materials (in a StandardMaterial3D tick UV1 → Triplanar and World Triplanar, so a texture tiles by the metre whatever the room's size), and set its floor kind (wood, stone, tile, carpet, dirt, metal, water) for the footsteps. Its id is an area: a step or a moment can wait for "the player enters kitchen".

Two rooms side by side: set them a wall's thickness apart (12 cm unless you change wall) and turn off one room's wall where they meet, so the wall isn't doubled. In the sample the hall keeps its east wall, the kitchen has none on its west, and the kitchen's door sits in the hall's wall. Where rooms meet, their walls share the corner by themselves: a room's north or south wall stops inside the other room's wall instead of running on to its face, and two rooms' floors meet on the middle of the wall between them instead of overlapping under it, so no thin line flickers down a wall or across a doorway as you move.

Doors

An HSDoor on a room's wall cuts its own opening: drag it along the wall and the hole follows. It swings away from the player. Locked by an item's id keeps it shut until the player has that item (or flag:name, a fact). It can have a peephole and a latch (a thumb turn to lock it from inside). Put a door model in as its child and it wears that instead of the plain leaf.

Doorways and windows with no door are the room's openings: a side, an offset along the wall, a width, a height and a sill. A window folds out more (A window): glass (a pane; the rain runs down it when it rains), daylight (the sky's light coming in by the time of day, a soft light just inside: on only where you want it, since you light the rest yourself), its shaft (that light's beam in the haze with dust drifting in it) and A car passing (headlights sliding past outside now and then; a moment's Sweep fires one). A room's leaks are water running down one of its walls: the side, along the wall, how high it comes in, its width and the seconds between drops; the wall is wet in a band below it and the drops fall to the floor.

Where the player starts

The panel's first category, Player, has three cards:

  • HSPlayerStart, the Player start: where the game begins in this level. A figure and an arrow in the editor (nothing in the game): the player stands there at their eye height and looks along the arrow.
  • The Test start (a Player start with start for test): where you begin while you test. F6 on the level's scene, and Play from here on it, begin there; the game itself never does. Put one in front of the part you're working on, with what you'd have by then.
  • HSCheckpoint, a checkpoint: an area that autosaves each time the player walks into it (the pause menu's autosave).

A level has one start of each: once there is one, its card says One in House and a click selects it to move. With a start selected, This object sets which way they look (eight ways: north is the editor's −Z), what they have with them (the scene's items as chips: the flashlight, a key; + Add an item), the player's own settings for the whole game (can run with Shift, can crouch with C, the eye height, their name: they're the HSGame's) and Play from here. In the sample the Test start is by the bedroom door with the bedroom key, so the bedroom can be tried without finding the key first.

Areas, spots and paths

  • HSTrigger, an area: a box the player walks into (orange in the editor, invisible in the game). A step or a moment waits for it by its id. Safe: a watching character can't see the player inside.
  • HSSpot, a spot: where a quest puts the player, where a character stands or is sent (with no Player start, the level's start or a spot named Start is where the player begins). It faces the way its arrow points (its +Z).
  • HSPath, a path a character walks: select it and draw points with Godot's path tools (Add Point, then click the floor), or give it HSSpot children you drag around. Mode: loop, there and back, or once; waits at its points. An HSSpot point can also have a pose she holds while she waits there (look_left, look_right...), a clip she plays there (§5, Paths) and a hiding place she checks (a locker's id: whoever hides in it is found).

Your own models and lights

Drop any model into the level. If it has collision it stops the player (Godot's own physics); if you want the player to do something with it, give it a part (next section). Every OmniLight3D and SpotLight3D in a level belongs to the story by its node's name: light switches flip the lights in their room, a power cut turns them off, a state or a moment's Light finds them. A light with "candle" or "fire" in its name keeps burning in a power cut. Light the level however you like; the look plugin's light nodes work as usual.

4Things to use: parts

Any model can be something the player does something with. Select your model in the scene and click what it is: a card in the panel (Make it this), or the Inspector's HorrorStudio box at the top (What is this?: the parts its name suggests, and Make it a… with every part by category). Or drag the card onto your model, in the 3D view or the Scene tree. The part takes your model's place and your model goes inside it: it's a thing now, an HSThing, with an id. Ctrl+Z undoes it, and This object's Make it plain again takes it off.

A thing's use says what the player does with it, and the Inspector shows only that use's fields; give it a display name for the cursor ("Read · Letter").

UseWhat the player does, and what to fill in

Hidden at start keeps a thing out of the level until the story shows it (a state, or a moment's Show): the key that turns up later, the figure for a jumpscare. Solidity says whether it stops the player (furniture does by default; a key or a note doesn't). Until you put a model in, a thing shows a stand-in box.

The little things

Kettle, Cup, Teapot, Pot, Hob, Candle, Cigarette, Lighter, Phone, Tap, TV, Clock, Fridge and more: parts too, with a life of their own. A kettle boils by itself once switched on, a cup goes cold, a candle burns down; a click does what it offers now (switch it on, pour, answer). Each comes with its effect points as markers (a kettle's spout, led, lid and switch; a cigarette's tip; a candle's flame), placed where they'd be on a thing of its size. Drag them onto your model: the steam comes out where you put the spout. life state is how it starts; the story can change it (a state, or a moment's State). A candle's life light is the light it gives: lit while it burns, out when it's blown out.

5Characters

An HSActor is a character. Put her model in as a child: a Character Studio export (Content/Studio/People/<name>/export/<name>.glb, copied into the project) or any rigged model. Without one a stand-in figure shows. She faces her +Z.

Or leave her empty and type the character's name in studio character: the game dresses her in that export with the Character Studio's own skin and eyes. install copies the export into the project (game/people/), so run it again after you set one. Kimodo motions need the model child, since they go onto its skeleton.

  • Behaviour: how she starts: idle (stands where you put her), sit (on a seat), path (walks an HSPath), patrol (several paths in turn), or off (not there until the story brings her). Face player turns her to them. Later the story's states move her (§8).
  • Sees player: she watches; in her cone and range the suspicion meter fills, and when it's full the player is caught (a step or a moment can wait for it).
  • Talk: someone the Director file has conversations for takes a click whether this is ticked or not (the cursor says Talk) and plays the one the story offers now (§8); tick it for a click with no conversation behind it.
  • Idle motion, walk motion: two of her moves (below): what she plays standing and walking. Her other moves (running, sitting down, sitting, getting up, talking, listening, looking round) are set on the Animate tab; This object (the Build tab) says how many she has.
  • Cast: who she is in the story when that isn't her id (a desk game's character placed in two places is both of them).
  • Expression: the face she rests in, a face clip from the project's faces/ (a loop, from faces/loops/); the Animate tab sets it too. The story puts other faces on her (a line's face, a state's, a moment's A face on someone). Her model needs MHR's face shapes: the plain MHR body has them, and install bakes them into a Character Studio character's copy (game/people/).
  • Hidden at start keeps her out of the level until the story shows her; solid off lets the player walk through her; speed is her walking pace.

Animations and faces: the Animate tab

A game's animations live in two folders of the project, both empty in a new game: animations/ for the body (Kimodo's .npz files as Kimodo saves them, or Godot animations, .res/.tres) and faces/ for the face (face clips, the loops in faces/loops/). Copy in what the game needs (the tab's Open animations/ and Open faces/ open them in your file manager): a file dropped in shows up as a card on the panel's Animate tab within a couple of seconds. A folder named after a character's id (animations/mari/) holds clips made for her.

  • The cards are grouped by what the clip's name says it is: idle, walk, run, sit, stand up, talk, listen, look & search, reactions, scares (mari_sit_reading.npz is a sit, jumpscare_lunge.npz a scare); anything else is under Other. Each says its length and whether it loops.
  • Select a character and click a card, or drag the card onto her in the 3D view or the Scene tree: a small menu asks what it's for: one of her moves (Her idle, Her walk, Her sitting…, the one its name suits marked ✓), Hers, for the story to play (a moment's Animate can then play it), or Look at it on her.
  • A Kimodo clip given to her is made on her own skeleton (the Character Studio's MHR, Kimodo's own, a Mixamo character or any model with Godot's humanoid bone map) and kept in animations/_made/<her id>/; a Godot animation is used as it is. Walks and runs are made in place (her path moves her).
  • Look at it on her plays it on her in the 3D view, and a face card's Look at it on her face plays a face on her face shapes, both at once if you like: the body and the face never fight (the body moves her bones, the face her face shapes). Nothing is saved; ■ Stop the preview puts her back (saving does too).
  • Her moves are listed on the right while she's selected: drop a card on one, or click it to pick from her clips and the library's that suit it; Fill the empty ones from the library gives each empty move the clip whose name suits it, hers first.
  • On the Face side a click makes a face Her resting face, and Make face clips (the Face page) opens the page that makes them (below).

Her moves in the game. She plays them by what she's doing, so you never have to say when:

Idlestanding (and at her spot)
Walk, Rungoing somewhere: a path, a patrol, the story sending her (Run when she's set to move fast, 2.2 m/s or more)
Sit down, Sitting, Stand upa seat: the way in once, the loop while she sits, the way out once (she gets up before she walks off)
Talk, Listenin a conversation: while her line is up, while you answer her
Look roundat a path's stop whose pose is look, search or check
Caughtwhen she catches the player (her sight's meter fills, a hiding place she checks has them): once, turned to them

An empty move plays another of hers, so the game always plays: Run plays her Walk; Sitting, Talk, Listen and Look round play her Idle; Sit down, Stand up and Caught are skipped. A clip whose name says it happens once (sit_down, stand_up, get_into_bed) isn't looped.

Who's where in each quest: On stage

Every quest in the Story tab has ON STAGE above its steps: a row per character saying what she's doing while it's on. Pick each blue word:

  • What she does: stands at, sits on (a spot or a seat), walks to, then stands or then sits, walks a path, patrols paths, or isn't there.
  • Doing: a clip of hers to play there (reading on the sofa); her moves plays her idle or sitting.
  • Face: the face she rests in during it.
  • Talks: the conversation she offers in this quest, or not now: she's busy.
  • …: the in-between's own clips (walks with, gets up with, arrives with) and faces you.

A line holds until a later quest changes it, so you only write what changes: a quest without a line for her shows the one carried to it in grey (as in “Intro”: stands at Mari's place), and Change it here starts one from it. Her conversation choice isn't carried: it belongs to its quest. When the quest moves on, the in-between plays by itself: if she was sitting she gets up (her Stand up), walks to her new place (her Walk) and sits down there (her Sit down) if that's what the new line says. Show on stage moves everyone in the 3D view to where the quest has them, doing it, until you press ■ Back to the scene (nothing is saved; saving puts them back too). Story Flow's quest cards show each line as a chip (Mari · stands at Mari's place).

A walk to another room goes through the doors. The game knows which room every place is in and which door joins which rooms, so a walk from the bedroom to the kitchen goes to the bedroom's door, through it, along the hall, through the kitchen's door and on to her place, by the fewest doors. Inside a room she walks straight, so keep big furniture off the line between a door and the places she's sent to, or send her by a path.

Paths and their stops

A path (HSPath, §4) is drawn in the 3D view with Godot's own path tools (select it, Add Point, click the floor), or as HSSpot children dragged where she should stop. She walks it with her Walk (her Run when she's fast) and, at each stop, waits as long as the stop says doing what it says:

  • Select the path: This object (the Build tab) lists its stops, each with a wait (seconds) and a clip to play there (searching the cupboards, looking out of the window); a stop with a clip and no wait plays the clip once through, then walks on.
  • Or drag a card from the Animate tab onto a stop in the 3D view or the Scene tree: it becomes that stop's clip, made for whoever walks the path.
  • A stop whose pose is look, search or check plays her Look round when it has no clip of its own.

Reactions and scares

The Reactions category (on the Build tab, and with the Story tab's ready-made moments) makes someone react to the player: Turns to look at you, Flinches, Screams, Points at you, Waves. Drop one into a room from the Build tab (an area there), or select an area or a door and click it in the Story tab (or drag it from there onto one): a moment, when the player walks in (or uses the door), in which whoever's nearest (of the characters in the game from the start) plays her clip for it, turned to the player, then goes back to what she was doing:

  • Her clip is found by its name: one of hers with flinch, startle, scream, point, wave... in it, else the library's, made hers. With none she only turns to look at you. A face with a fitting name (shock, fear, smile) goes on her face too, and Screams brings a scream from the sound shelf.
  • Swap her clip by dropping: drag a card from the Animate tab onto the same area or door, and the moment's clip becomes that one, made for her.
  • Change any word after in the Story tab: who, the clip, whom she turns to (turned to: the player, someone or something), for how long. A command Animate someone does the same anywhere: she's held where she is while it plays (she stops walking), then carries on.

The jumpscare (Scares) has more words than its figure: a clip the figure plays while it's there (a lunge: give the figure a body and the clip on the Animate tab, as you would a character), its face, and its own sound instead of the big sting. With a clip, the scare lasts as long as it unless you say for. In the sample the figure lunges with a face of its own and the short sting.

Caught: give a character a Caught move (above) and she plays it, turned to the player, whenever she catches them; what happens next is the story's (a moment when the player is caught).

Cutscenes in the level

A cutscene here never takes the game away: no black bars, nothing to skip, the player stays in their body and the scene happens round them. Open a moment in the Story tab and press Not a cutscene to make it Played as a cutscene:

  • The view held on someone, a spot or a thing: as it starts the view turns onto it; while it plays the player can look round within a cone, and when they look away the view is drawn gently back to it.
  • Closer: a little closer or closer leans the eye in toward it and narrows the view a little (a held focus); both ease in and out. The lens never changes otherwise.
  • Walking: slowed (the default), standing still, or free.
  • Let go after at most: the safety release. The view is let go when the moment's THEN is over, or when what it's held on is gone (hidden, out of the level), at an ending or a new level, and in any case after these many seconds (20), whatever happens: a glitch can never keep the player in it.

Its timeline shows the THEN as rows: The view, a row for each character it names with what she does in order (walks, clips, faces, lines), and the Lines. Add a clip or a face from her row's + a clip or a face… (it goes at the end; the THEN plays in that order, a clip waited for), or drop a card from the Animate tab on her row. Lines follow one another, each given the time to be read. Play it here plays the game from that quest, standing by where it happens.

The same as a command anywhere: Hold the view (a cutscene) on someone, and an empty one to let go.

Face clips from a video: the Face page

Face clips are made from a short video of a face (a close-up, like your takes). On the Animate tab's Face side, Make face clips (the Face page) opens the studio's Face page in your browser, already pointed at this project. (Without Godot: python tools/facecap/serve.py, then http://127.0.0.1:8797; it's in the launcher's Pages too.)

  1. Import a video. It's read on your machine, every frame's expression (the head's own movement left out); it takes a few seconds a clip.
  2. The preview plays the video beside a character wearing the face, in sync: the default MHR, or one of your Character Studio characters (Character). Set what the face at rest is (Neutral), how strong (Strength), how long it takes (Length) and its name, then Accept: it's in the studio's expression library (Content/Studio/Expressions, the same one the desk's Expressions tab shows).
  3. Click a card to open it again: rename it, retune it, or cut a loop from it (drag across the strip under the preview for the part to hold, Save the range as a loop): a loop plays over and over, its end blended into its start, for a face she rests in.
  4. For Godot: the project's folder (filled in when you came from the Animate tab), tick the faces the game needs, Send. Each becomes a face clip in the project's faces/ (a loop in faces/loops/) and a card on the Animate tab's Face side within a couple of seconds.

The same from a terminal, by name (no names: the whole library):

python tools/godot_director.py faces ~/Games/MyGame pout_and_glance pout_and_glance_loop

If the story animates someone with a clip she hasn't been given, the panel's Test page says so, and names the Animate tab when the clip is in animations/.

6The story: the Director file

The scene is the world; the Director file is the story. It's one file, director/director.json, and nothing in the scene knows about it: the story names the scene's things by id ("when the player picks up bedroom_key", "Mari walks to bedroom_spot"). You write it in the panel's Story tab (§8) or in the Director Manager (§9), in the same words; the dock's Director tab shows it as it will play, and the game reads it each time it starts.

Night 1 · 23:40→The key→ A drink→Mari→ The bedroom→Something in the hall→ MorningorAsleep

Quests, one at a time

The game is its quests, played one after another, in order. A quest has a title, an objective (top left) and steps: the to-do list under it, each done by something the player does (reads the letter, picks up the key, walks into the bedroom, hides in the wardrobe) and maybe failed by another (takes too long, gives the wrong thing).

Every game starts with its Intro. A new story's first quest is the Intro: what happens as the game opens (a chapter card, a thought, a look round), set out in its As it starts commands; it has no list and ends once they're over, and the next quest begins. The sample's intro shows Night 1 · 23:40, then the thought "Mari's out. She said she'd leave the bedroom key somewhere."

Not every quest is a to-do list. Untick Its steps as a to-do list on the screen and its steps still count, unseen: nothing written at the top left, the player finds the way by the story (and by a step's marker, below). The sample's quest Mari is one: no objective, no list, just the marker over her.

  • A step only counts while its quest is on. "Turn the light on, then off": if they did it before that quest started, it doesn't tick by itself; they do it again. (For what was done before, ask a fact instead, §7.)
  • A step done stays on the list, crossed out with a line (a failed one in red).
  • A quest ends when all its steps are done, on its first step done (the night: hide or sleep, whichever they do), when what happens as it starts is over (an intro, a little scene), or never by itself (a moment says when).
  • As it starts: a chapter card before it if you like (a title, the time, the view dark, an autosave), where the player starts at, a checkpoint, a time limit and what happens when it runs out, and commands to run (as it starts, when it's done).
  • A quest can be a round of gameplay instead of steps (a round of gameplay instead of steps in the Manager): hide and seek, a chase to a door, sneaking past her, holding still... with its seconds, its rule (the one line a child could say), its warning, who hunts and where to hide, and what each result does. Its result ends the quest and is a fact, result:q_hide:found, for what comes next.

Talking to someone

A step can be talking to someone ("Talk to Mari"): Talk to someone… at the top of WHEN in the Story tab (talk to… in the Manager), then who, and when it counts:

  • when she's clicked: as soon as she starts a conversation, whatever she says;
  • when she's heard out: once a conversation with her has been heard to its end, whichever it was;
  • when “You found it.” is heard out: one conversation of hers in particular, named by its title or its first line. The sample's third quest is this one: Talk to Mari ticks once she's said “You found it.” and you've answered her.

The same works for a moment's WHEN (when the player talks to her, something happens), and as an ONLY IF (they've talked to Mari, they've heard out Mari's “You found it.”). Which conversation she offers is up to her conversations' own offered when (§8): a step that waits for a particular one ticks only once it's the one she offers.

A marker over what it needs

Any step can show a marker in the game over what it needs: a small flat diamond on a short stem, pointing down at the thing, the person or the place and bobbing slowly, in the HUD's own accent colour. Tick Show a marker over it in the game on the step in the Story tab (a marker over it in the Manager). It hangs over what the step's WHEN names (for a talk, the person), or pick what it's over. It's optional: use it where words would be clumsy (a talk: no "Talk to Mari" needed, just the marker over her), and leave it off where the player should look for themselves. It shows only while the step is open, fades out past 30 m, and grows softer up close, where the cursor's prompt says what it is.

Giving something to someone

A step can wait for the player to give something to someone (Something is given to someone: the mug to Mari). While that step is on, clicking her with something in the hand offers it to her instead of talking. The right thing ticks the step and she takes it; for anything else, the wrong thing: her answer ("Nah, that's not what I want."), and the step can fail too if you tick it.

What comes next

When a quest is done, the next one in the list starts, unless it has ways: each a condition (§7) and where it goes, a quest or an ending; the first that holds wins. That's how the story branches, and how a secret ending or an easter egg waits for what the player did along the way.

An ending is a title and its words, and a kind: good, bad, neutral, secret, or a death, which offers a retry from the last checkpoint.

The whole story as a flowchart: Story Flow

Click Story Flow at the top of the editor, beside 2D, 3D and Script. Every quest and ending is a card, numbered as the Story tab lists them, and the arrows are what comes next, the way the game plays it. Each way out is a line on its card in its own colour: then (blue), if… (violet: only when that holds, the first that does), otherwise (grey-blue: when none of its ifs hold) and out of time (amber: its time limit ran out). A long story wraps onto rows like lines of text; an arrow going on to the next row runs round the cards.

  • Click a card: on the right, how the player gets there (every arrow into it: its prerequisites, lit on the chart) and where it goes. The first card says the game starts there; a card nothing leads to says the player never gets there.
  • Double-click a card (or Open it in the Story tab): it opens in the panel's Story tab below, with its steps and moments.
  • Drag cards where you like: their places are kept with the story (Ctrl+Z puts them back). Lay it out again forgets them; Fit the view shows it all.

A game brought in from the desk shows its scenes and choices as cards too (marked DESK), and its trigger cutscenes hung off the quests they listen during; those are changed in the Director Manager.

Setting the story up in it, without the detail (the steps and moments stay in the Story tab):

  • A new quest: right-click the chart where you want it (or + Quest, + Ending) and type its title on the card (F2 types it again). It goes at the end of the list; what was last keeps going where it went.
  • What comes next: drag a way's dot (on the right of a card) onto another card. Dropped in empty space, it offers a new quest or ending there, or Take this way away.
  • A branch: click the card, then + A way: if… on the right and pick what has to hold (the same words as everywhere: they've read letter). Pick where it goes beside it, or drag its dot onto a card. Each way there has its condition's chips, where it goes, ↑ ↓ (the first that holds wins) and ✕.
  • Delete takes a card away (asked first): the ways into it go on where it went, a quest's own moments go with it.
  • Number them by the flow puts the list in the order the story reaches them (the numbers on the cards and in the Story tab), and the game plays exactly the same.

Everything is on Ctrl+Z.

Easter eggs

An easter egg is a hidden quest: nothing on the screen announces it, and it isn't in the quests' order. It's found while one of its quests is on (none named: any of them), when something happens (FOUND WHEN: they put the cat ornament down, they walk into the bedroom), only if its ONLY IF holds. It plays what it plays (a jump scare, an animation, a thought), once a playthrough, and then either the game goes on where it was, or it jumps ahead to a later quest or an ending: a skip, the quests in between left behind. Finding one is the fact done:<egg>, for a moment's ONLY IF, another egg's, or an achievement (every egg found).

  • In Story Flow: + Easter egg (or a right-click, A new easter egg here) makes a gold card. Drag a quest's easter eggs found during it dot onto it for the quests it's found during; drag its then dot onto a card for its jump. Click it: on the right where it's found, its FOUND WHEN, ONLY IF and what it plays, and then.
  • In the panel's Story tab: Easter eggs under the quests, + Add an easter egg; the same rows, DURING picked by name.
  • In the Director Manager: + Easter egg on the Quests tab.

7Facts, conditions, achievements

As the player plays, the Director writes down what they've done as facts. You never declare one: they're kept with the run (a save keeps them), and anything in the story can ask for them.

FactWritten when the player...
used:kettleuses a thing (clicks it, whatever it does)
picked:bedroom_keypicks up an item
read:letterreads a note
entered:kitchen, left:kitchenwalks into, or out of, a room or an area
talked:mari, talked:t_openclicks someone and she starts a conversation (her, and which one)
heard:mari, heard:t_openhears a conversation to its end (her, and which one)
answered:t_open:yespicks an answer
gave:mug:marigives something to someone
step:find_key, failed:give_mugticks a step; fails one
done:q_key, ending:e_morningfinishes a quest; reaches an ending

caught:mari, dropped:mugis caught by someone watching; puts something down

A condition asks about them. It's one or more of: a fact (or not a fact), a quest that's on now, a quest done, a step ticked, an item held, a counter, karma, an ending reached; all of them, or any one. Conditions are everywhere the story decides something: a quest's ways, a moment's if, a conversation's offered when, a line's said, an answer's offered, a state's while, an achievement's when. "If they read the letter and haven't opened the bedroom yet" is read:letter and not done:q_bedroom.

Achievements

An achievement is unlocked when its condition holds, and kept as the fact achievement:<id>. Its id is the name Steam will know it by; its hint shows before it's unlocked unless it's hidden. Criteria can be anything a condition asks, including facts the player never sees as quests (name your own in the Manager's Facts, ticked hidden). The games are made ready for Steam now; Steam itself (GodotSteam) comes later.

8Moments, conversations, states

In Godot: the Story tab

The panel's Story tab writes the quests, their steps, the moments and the endings right in Godot, each as a sentence, in the Director Manager's words. On the left are the quests in the order they're played, each with its steps (ticked and crossed out while the game runs from the editor) and the endings; click one to edit it. In the middle is what happens in that quest: its steps (WHEN Mug is given to Mari, tick “Give Mari the mug”) and its moments. Click one to open it and pick each blue word:

  • WHEN: what happens (the player enters, uses, reads, picks up, is given…), and what to (the scene's own areas, things, people); Talk to someone… (§6); or once a condition becomes true.
  • ONLY IF: what must already be so, as words (they've read letter). + and… adds one from everything the scene and the story have; click one to turn it into its opposite (they haven't read letter), ✕ takes it away, and and/or between them says whether all or any must hold.
  • THEN: what happens, in order: a scare, a thought, a sound, a light, someone walking off… + Then… adds one; ↑ ↓ move it, ✕ takes it away.
  • How often: Just once, or Every time.

Under it, It reads as says the whole moment as one sentence. Try this moment plays the game from this quest as a test, with what it asks for already done and you standing by where it happens; Show it in the 3D view selects what its WHEN names (press F over the view to frame it). On the right are ready-made moments (a jumpscare, a knock at a door, the lights dying (all at once, or one by one toward you), a light dimming for a few seconds (the light nearest where it lands), a door slamming, a whisper, a thought, the flashlight flickering, and the reactions, §5): click one to add it onto what's selected (an area, a room, a door, a thing), or drag it onto an area or a door in the 3D view. Every change is saved at once, and Ctrl+Z takes it back.

Moments: when, if, then

A moment is how you make things happen: jumpscares, thoughts, sounds, lights, doors, someone arriving. It reads as a sentence:

When the player enters the kitchen's doorway, if they've read the letter, then the figure shows for a second, they think "What was that?", and saw_figure is set.
  • When: anything that happens (the player enters, picks up, uses, reads, looks at, hides, is caught; a step is ticked; a door is locked; the power goes...), picked from the same palette as the desk's; or or once…: the moment a condition becomes true.
  • If: a condition (§7). Only during: quests (none: the whole game). Once a playthrough, or every time.
  • Then: the commands, the desk's own: a jumpscare, a glimpse, a knock, the lights dying, a door slamming behind, a thought, a sound, music, show or hide something, a door, a light, the power, a little thing's state, a character sent somewhere or animated (a reaction), a fact set, an item given or taken, karma, a counter...

A light (THEN, Light): pick one of your lights by its name, or the room, and set it on, off, flicker, dim or normal. A dim has how bright (0 to 1); a flicker can take one of the lamps' flickers (a fluorescent, a dying tube, a candle...); fading over makes the change take that long. Give it for seconds and it comes back as it was by itself (a lamp the player switched off stays off); without it the change stays. On and normal bring back the light's own brightness and its own flicker after a dim or a flicker. So "when the player walks into the hall, if the key's quest is on, the hall lamp dims to 0.2 for 4 seconds" is one moment: drop A light dims in the hall, pick the quest under Only during, and change the light or the numbers if you like. A state's light (§7) goes back as the level has it once its condition stops holding.

Conversations

Each person has her conversations, in order. Clicking her plays the first whose offered when holds, so she says what fits where the story is: "Not now. Find the key." before the key, "Could you bring me something to drink?" during the errand, her question after it. A once only conversation goes once it's been heard out. It plays live: the player can look around while she talks.

Its lines in order: who says it (her, someone else, or the player's thought) and the words; a line can be said only if a condition holds, wear her face as she says it (an expression, played once over the face she rests in), and run commands as it starts. A line with answers is a question (two at most): each answer's label, when it's offered, the facts it sets, karma, her reply, and then: the conversation goes on, or the story goes to a quest or an ending (a wrong answer's death). Answers are never timed: the player must choose.

States: what the world looks like

A state says what the world looks like while a condition holds: a person's face (the expression she rests in), at a spot, what she does (stands, sits, walks to a spot, walks a path, patrols), whether she's watching for the player, her motion, seen or not; a thing shown or hidden; a little thing's state; a door locked or open; a light on, off, flickering or dim. The Director works the states out again after every change and every level entered (a load, a retry), so the world always matches the story; a later state's settings win over an earlier one's. In the sample, once Mari's been talked to, she walks to the bedroom door.

Coverage: what's missing

Coverage lists what won't play right (a quest that never finishes, a way that goes nowhere, a moment with no when, a quest or an ending nothing leads to, a fact asked for that nothing sets), what the story names that the scene hasn't got (a typo, or something still to build), and what the scene has that the story never names, by kind: the doors no state locks, the things no step asks for. A good list of what's still to do.

Saves and where the next play starts

A new game always starts from nothing; delete a save here to clear it (one, or all of them). The next play starts at sets where the next F5 begins, once: a quest, with the facts it should have (what the quests before it would have set), as the dock's ▶ Here does.

11Kimodo, there and back

Make a character's motion in Kimodo, in the room it'll play in, and bring it back as her own animation.

  1. Select the character (or a spot) and press Send the selected character's room. The room she's in and the rooms next to it go to Kimodo as its stage, seen from where she stands, scaled to her height. (whole level sends all of it.)
  2. Open Kimodo starts Kimodo's demo and opens it in your browser. The room is in its Stage panel.
  3. Make the motion there and save it (Exports, NPZ, Download; or Send to Studio).
  4. Back in Godot, Look for motions lists the saved .npz files (from the project's kimodo/motions, the studio's Saved/Kimodo and your Downloads). Pick one.
  5. Name it, tick in place for a walk (the path moves her) and loops for an idle or a walk, choose whether it's her idle or walk, and press Import onto the selected character.

The motion goes into the project's animation library (animations/<her id>/, a card on the Animate tab, §5) and onto her own skeleton, the fingers too (the Character Studio's MHR, Kimodo's own, a Mixamo character, or any model imported with Godot's humanoid bone map), kept in animations/_made/<her id>/, and starts playing. In the game her idle motion loops while she stands and her walk motion while she walks; a state can give her a motion, and a moment's motion command plays one once. A Kimodo .npz dropped straight into animations/ works the same way, from the Animate tab.

The studio folder (the one with tools/kimodo.py) is filled in when the project is made; set Python to how you run the studio's tools (python3, uv run python...). Kimodo itself must be set up first (python setup/setup.py kimodo).
