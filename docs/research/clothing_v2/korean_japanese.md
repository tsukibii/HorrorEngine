# Korean and Japanese smart casual, uniforms, dresses and suits: what can be had ready-made

Research date 2026-11-11. The user, after the first fit pictures: "Can you see if you can find some more
korean/japanese smart casual designs, uniforms, dresses, suits."

Network note: MakeHuman's packs are counted from the library note beside this one (`tools_and_assets.md`, which read
the pack pages at source) and from the packs' own thumbnails. sketchfab.com, cgtrader.com, booth.pm and vroid.com
could not be opened from this session, so everything about them here is **from search summaries** and has to be
confirmed on the page before a garment is used: above all each model's licence line.

## 1. Takeaway

- **The free library covers the plain half of this well and the specifically Japanese and Korean half not at all.**
  Suits, office wear, shirts and ties, cardigans, slacks, pleated plaid skirts, sheath dresses, oxfords and mary janes
  are all there. A sailor uniform, a gakuran, a hanbok, a kimono or a yukata is not (there is a qipao, and a bathrobe
  named kimono).
- **The best lead outside it is Style3D's free garments on Sketchfab.** Style3D makes a professional garment design
  tool; its account publishes modern, realistic garments made in it, and the search summaries show most under **CC
  Attribution**: several Japanese school uniforms ("JK School Uniform", 1 to 4), business suits for women and men,
  shirt-and-skirt sets, a vest and pleated skirt suit, dresses, coats, hoodies. They are the East Asian ready-to-wear
  look the user's references have. Two costs: they are heavy as published (158,500 triangles for one uniform, 836,500
  for one suit set, as listed) and they come without the body they were draped on, so they need thinning and a
  placement step before the fit (`fit_test.md` §5, last point).
- **VRoid** has school uniforms free and usable commercially, but anime-styled and made in a Windows and macOS app.
- **No free hanbok, kimono or yukata** turned up under CC0 or CC-BY. Paid ones start at about $10 a garment under
  royalty-free licences; the same fit would take them.
- **Until one of those is brought in**, the looks can be put together from library pieces in our own fabrics
  (`fit_styles.jpg`), and the tailor's own jeogori, chima and hanbok designs remain the only Korean garments we have.

## 4. The looks put together from library pieces

`fit_styles.jpg`, fitted by the test's algorithm and re-dressed in the tailor's fabrics:

1. **A blazer-type school uniform**: punkduck's uniform jacket in navy flannel, Elvaerwyn's pleated miniskirt in a
   grey and navy plaid, the system's black shoes and socks. It reads as one at the player's distance. What a real one
   has that this lacks: a shirt collar and a ribbon at the neck, a crest (a decal), a shorter and more fitted jacket.
2. **A gakuran-like set**: the same jacket and Mindfront's trousers in black twill. The jacket has a turn-down collar,
   not a stand collar.
3. **A camel coat over a cream sweater and dark trousers**: the Korean street look's pieces are there, but two library
   garments worn together cross each other (the layering the test doesn't do yet).
4. **The CC0 men's suit** as it comes.

So the library gets the silhouettes of uniforms, suits and office wear now, and Style3D's garments are where the
actual Japanese and Korean cuts would come from.
