# The prop generator: small props from Python in Blender

`python tools/propgen.py` makes small props the way AssetsFactory's *Free Street Litter Sampler* says its were: "All
meshes and textures are generated by our own Python scripts in Blender. No image or 3D generator was used." A recipe
(a few lines of JSON) becomes a game-ready prop: a .glb for Godot, an .fbx, an .obj, a .blend, its textures (base
colour, ORM, normal) and a preview picture. The same recipe always gives the same prop; another seed gives another
one.

## 1. How the sampler was made

The itch.io page can't be reached from the cloud, so this comes from its note (quoted above) and its two pictures.
Every sign points to code, not photographs.

- **Shapes from measurements.** The pristine can, bottle and cups are perfectly round. They're turned from a profile:
  a 330 ml can is a real 66 mm by 115 mm can, with its domed base, necked shoulder and countersunk lid. The burger box
  is a tray swept round an outline. The paper is a subdivided sheet.
- **Damage by deformation, after the print.** The crushed cans keep both rims round while the middle buckles. The
  print folds exactly with the metal and paper, so the label was mapped on the undamaged shape and the mesh deformed
  afterwards. A photo scan would carry baked-in light and noisy geometry; these don't. The crumpled ball and the
  napkin look simulated (cloth-like), the cans look deformed by a function.
- **Labels drawn by code.** FIZZ, CLEARBROOK, VOLT and THE RIVERTON LEDGER are made-up brands in flat colours with
  simple patterns: stripes, dots, zigzags, bubbles, greeked small print. That's vector drawing with a font, then baked
  to textures. Probably Blender's own text objects and shader nodes baked with Cycles. We draw in numpy instead (§3),
  which is faster and the same on every machine.
- **Detail baked down.** Small game props (a few thousand triangles) with creases sharper than their mesh means a
  detailed copy baked into normal and occlusion maps.
- **One studio render.** A grey floor, a big soft light and Cycles. Our previews copy it.
- **"Written with the help of an AI coding assistant"** means the scripts were written with an assistant, not that
  an AI made the assets. That's the same arrangement as here.

**Photo to model (i2model)?** Not what they did: invented labels and perfect symmetry don't come from photos. A photo
*can* still drive a turned thing (a side view's silhouette gives the profile) or a print (your picture as the label:
`art: {"image": ...}`, §3). That's a possible later step (§9), not this tool's way.

## 2. Commands

```bash
python tools/propgen.py list                              # every recipe by family
python tools/propgen.py make soda_can_crushed             # one (or more) into Saved/Props/<name>/
python tools/propgen.py make soda_can --set art.brand=HANA --set crush.amount=0.9 --seed 7 --as hana_crushed
python tools/propgen.py all [family...]                   # every recipe, or a family's
python tools/propgen.py sheet [names...]                  # a contact sheet of the previews: Saved/Props/sheet.png
python tools/propgen.py library [names...]                # made props into the library as props/<name> (no Blender)
python tools/propgen.py godot [names...]                  # GDPROPS: imported by Godot 4.7, materials read back, pictured
python tools/propgen.py pbr                               # the PBR shelf's sets
python tools/propgen.py pbr-add Metal/brushed ~/Downloads/brushed_*.png   # a set onto the shelf
python tools/propgen.py check                             # PROPGEN
```

- **Blender.** It looks for `--blender PATH`, then `$BLENDER`, then `blender` on the PATH (your Omarchy machine), then
  Blender's Python module (`python tools/retarget.py setup`, the cloud's way). It runs in the background, so no window
  opens.
- **Graphics card.** Cycles uses it when it finds one (OptiX on the 3090); `--cpu` turns that off.
- **Options.** `--formats glb,fbx,obj,blend` (all four by default), `--tex 2048` (the texture size), `--no-preview`,
  `--samples N` (the bakes'), `--library` (also into the library).
- **What it makes.** `Saved/Props/<name>/` holds `<name>.glb`, `.fbx`, `.obj` + `.mtl`, `.blend`,
  `<name>_<material>_albedo.png`, `_orm.png` and `_normal.png`, `<name>_preview.png`, and `meta.json` (the resolved
  recipe, size, triangles, time).
- **Time.** About 20 to 40 s a prop on the cloud's 4 CPUs (half of it the preview); much less on the 3090.

## 3. A recipe

Recipes live in `Content/Studio/Props/presets.json`. A recipe names its family and what differs from that family's
defaults. `from` starts from another recipe, and every brand in it is made up.

```json
"soda_can_crushed": {"label": "the soda can stepped on", "from": "soda_can", "seed": 7,
   "crush": {"amount": 0.9, "twist": 12, "bend": 4, "buckle": 0.7, "wrinkle": 0.6},
   "wear": {"grime": 0.35, "dirt": 0.5, "scratches": 0.6, "dust": 0.25}}
```

Every family takes these:

| Key | What |
| --- | --- |
| `seed` | rolls the dice: dents, folds, crumples, wear, the small print |
| `tex` | texture size in pixels (1024 by default; 2048 for a close-up) |
| `art` | the print: `{"design": "<name>", ...its fields}`, or `{"image": "my_label.png"}` (your own picture over the island) |
| `wear` | 0-1 each: `grime`, `dirt` (gathers in creases from the baked occlusion), `dust` (on what faces up), `scratches`, `edges` (worn edges), `stains`, `fade` |
| `pose` | `stand`, `lie`, `auto` (the stablest face), `any` (a stable face by the seed), `upside`; `yaw` turns it, `down` picks which side lies down |
| `view` | the preview camera: `azim`, `elev`, `lens` |
| `relief`, `ao_gamma`, `sharp_angle` | fine-tuning: the print's emboss strength, the occlusion's depth, hard edges past that angle |

### Designs

A design draws a print from a few fields (`designs.py`). Each is a function on a Frame (one island in millimetres):

- **Drinks:** `soda`, `energy`, `beer`, `coffee_can` (Japanese text), `water`.
- **Cups:** `coffee_cup`, `sleeve`, `iced`.
- **Paper:** `newspaper` (page 1: masthead, headline, photo, columns; `page: 2`: classifieds), `newsprint`,
  `napkin`, `fries`, `receipt` (its `items` and `date`), `note` (`text` in `marker` or `typewriter`), `paper`.
- **Card:** `burger_box`, `kraft`, `card_plain`.
- **Wood and lacquer:** `wood_grain`, `lacquer`.
- **Glazes:** `glaze`, `sometsuke`, `speckled`, `jar_label`.

Fonts are in `tools/propgen/fonts/`: OFL and Apache fonts from Google Fonts, with their licences beside them. Only the
rendered textures ship in a game, never the fonts (the OFL leaves what they draw free). Name one by its key (`anton`,
`bebas`, `archivo`, `pacifico`, `lobster`, `fraktur`, `serif`, `serif_bold`, `typewriter`, `marker`, `mono`,
`bungee`, `righteous`, `rubik`, `gothic_jp` for Japanese) or by a .ttf path.

### Metal finishes (`finish.py`)

How bare metal was made and then used:

- `mirror`: polished to a mirror, a few hairline scratches.
- `polished`: bright, faint polishing marks.
- `satin`: a fine even grain.
- `brushed`: straight grain.
- `swirl`: the circular scratches of cutlery that's been washed and dried a thousand times.
- `scratched`: long random scratches (keys in a pocket).
- `hammered`: beaten dimples.
- `cast`: grainy and pitted.

On top of a finish, `age` lays `tarnish` (silver going warm and dull), `patina` (brass going brown, green in the
hollows) or `rust`. The metals: steel, silver, brass, copper, iron, nickel, gold, aluminium, chrome, bronze, or a
'#hex'.

### Wear

The wear layers go by each texel's place on the thing: its rest position (so the noise follows the material and has
no seams), its normal, and the occlusion and curvature baked from the detailed mesh. Dirt therefore settles into a
crushed can's folds, dust settles on what faces up, and scratches and worn edges show what's under the finish. What a
layer does depends on the material's kind:

- **Painted metal:** bare metal shows through.
- **Varnished wood:** the pale wood shows.
- **Card:** the fibres show.
- **Cloth:** it's rubbed lighter.

## 4. How a prop is made (`tools/propgen/`)

1. **The family builds the thing** (`families/<name>.py`).
   - First its islands: where each part's print goes, sized in millimetres and packed into the texture at one density
     (`build.Thing.pack`).
   - Then its meshes at rest: the game mesh, and a detailed one when it has detail the game mesh can't carry.
   - Shapes come from `mesh.py`:
     - `lathe`: a profile turned (cans, bottles, cups, ware).
     - `loft`: a profile swept round an outline, with holes and concave caps filled by constrained Delaunay (boxes).
     - `slab`: an outline cut from a sheet with rounded edges (cutlery, keys).
     - `sweep`: a section along a path (a mug's handle).
     - `sheet`, `extrude`.
2. **Deformers** (`deform.py`, `sim.py`) change only the vertices, from each vertex's own coordinates, so the game mesh
   and the detailed one deform alike:
   - `crush`: each ring of a wall keeps its length as the circle becomes a stadium, with buckles, twist and bend.
   - `dents`.
   - `displace`.
   - The paper simulation: position-based dynamics in numpy. The sheet can't stretch, its bends set into creases past a
     yield, its particles keep their distance, and it lies on the floor. It does random folds, a squeeze in a
     shrinking sphere, a fold along a line, a curl and a lie-down.
3. **Set down** (`deform.rest_pose`): the faces of its convex hull it can stand on (its centre of mass over the face),
   the one asked for, lowest point on the floor.
4. **Painted** (`paint.py`): shapes, patterns and text in millimetres per island, laying down colour, roughness,
   metalness, height and a normal (from a PBR set) together. Text comes from the font's own outlines through Blender's
   text objects; shapes are filled by the nonzero rule at 4 × 4 samples a pixel.
5. **Baked** (`bake.py`, Cycles): the detailed mesh's normals, ambient occlusion and curvature onto the game mesh's
   texture. A numpy rasterizer gives each texel its rest place, normal and tangent frame.
6. **Worn** (`surface.py`), its maps written (base colour, an ORM texture, an OpenGL normal map), its material made the
   way the glTF exporter carries it to Godot, exported, pictured (`stage.py`).

**Polycounts.** A game mesh keeps the shape. Sharp detail (crush folds, rounded edges, crimps, film folds, chips) lives
in its normal map, baked from a detailed copy. A crushed can is 10k triangles (the detail 84k), a spoon 2.3k (39k), a
key 1.7k (13k), a burger box 0.5k (17k). The cans and cups at 4k to 7k could go lower (`segments`) for far-away
litter.

## 5. Your own things, worn: `import`, and PBR sets

```json
"chair_worn": {"family": "import", "kit": "dining_chair", "seed": 3, "tex": 1024,
   "materials": {"*": {"kind": "wood"}, "kit_wood": {"pbr": "pbr/Wood/worn_planks", "tile": 0.45}},
   "dents": {"count": 12, "depth": 1.2, "radius": 18}, "detail": 6,
   "wear": {"edges": 0.7, "scratches": 0.6, "dirt": 0.45, "dust": 0.35, "grime": 0.3}}
```

- **The source:** `file` (a .glb, .fbx or .obj you made: furniture from your interiors), or `kit` (a kit preset, built
  by the kit's own code, `tools/kit/build.mjs`, with `kit_spec` for its numbers).
- **UVs:** `uv: auto` keeps them when they're one 0-1 layout, and makes new ones a material each (Smart UV Project)
  when they tile or are missing. The kit's meshes tile in metres, so they get new ones. `keep` or `new` forces it.
- **Materials,** by name (`*` for the rest):
  - `pbr`: a set off the shelf, laid by **triplanar projection at its real size** (`tile` metres a repeat), so it
    doesn't matter what the UVs are. Its normal map is turned into each texel's tangent frame.
  - `tint`, `color` / `rough` / `metal` (in place of what it had), `kind` (what wear does to it), `wear` (its own
    layers).
  - With none of these, a material keeps its own colour, and its own texture when its UVs are kept.
- **Dents:** pressed into a subdivided copy (`detail` mm edges) and baked onto your mesh, which keeps its own triangles.
- **PBR sets:** the shelf is the desk's (`assets/pbr/`, docs/ASSETS.md §6).
  - **To add one:** `propgen.py pbr-add NAME FILES...` (maps by their names' last word: albedo, diff, normal, nor_gl,
    rough...), the shelf page's tools, or drop its folder or .zip into `assets/pbr/`.
  - **To use one:** `propgen.py pbr` lists the refs to put in `pbr`.
  - **On an island:** any family's island can lay a set too (`Frame.pbr` in a design).
- **Test sets:** `python tools/pbr.py demo` puts five made-up sets on the shelf. The chair above wears their planks; a
  real wood-grain set reads properly.

## 6. Into the games and Godot

- **Into the library:** `propgen.py library [names]` (or `make ... --library`) puts each made prop there as
  `props/<name>`.
  - The mesh goes to `Content/Studio/Library/meshes/props/` (generated, so git ignores it, like the kit's).
  - Its size and triangles go to `index/props.json`. The index ships, so a story can name a prop before it's made
    here, as with the Base Mesh.
  - Its line goes to `LEDGER.json` (ours).
- **In a story:** `"shape": "asset props/soda_can_crushed"` (the mesh at its real size) or `"art": "props/<name>"` on
  a block. The story check and `cc0.py needs` know them.
- **In Godot:** the Godot export copies them into `game/meshes/props/`. Tested on a scratch copy of the Sample game:
  STORY ok, GDEXPORT ok, the four meshes in `house.tscn`.
- **What Godot reads** (`propgen.py godot`, GDPROPS):
  - every surface's base colour, ORM and normal map;
  - the PET and glass see-through (alpha blend);
  - sizes as made.
- **The picture:** `Saved/Props/_godot/godot_props.png`, every prop in a row under a light.
- **Draw calls:** each prop is one mesh with one or two materials.

## 7. TestRoom001's things: what fits a generator

**Made here now:**

- `key_brass_pickup`: `key` (house, brass) and `key_skeleton`.
- The notes (`code_note`, `annex_note`, `painting_note`, `library_diary`'s pages): `sheet` with `note`.
- The mug and kitchen things: `ware` and `utensil`.
- The tins (`tin_a`, `tin_b`, `tin_c`): `can` today (a food tin with a paper label is a small step, §9).
- The torch, the batteries and the fuse are turned things too, and suit `lathe` (§9).
- The box and the crate: `box` (card; a wooden crate is §9).
- The bin and the bucket: turned, so §9.
- The die: a rounded cube with pips, §9.

**Worn, not made:** the kit's furniture (sofa, bed, chairs, shelves, desk, cabinets) and the scene builder's things.
They're designed in the kit and kept there; `import` with `kit: <preset>` wears them (dents, scratches, worn edges,
dirt, PBR sets) without changing their shape.

**Doesn't fit a generator:**

- **The porcelain doll and the plushie:** characters with faces and soft anatomy. They want a sculpt, or the Character
  Studio's tools.
- **The towel:** cloth draped over a rail needs a collider the paper simulation doesn't have. A folded towel could be
  a `sheet`.
- **The mop's head:** strands; the stick and bucket are easy.
- **The kit's living little things** (kettle, hob, saucepan, rice cooker, clock, phone, ashtray with a burning
  cigarette): they have sockets and lives the desk and Godot drive (steam at the spout, a clock's hands), so they stay
  kit things. A generated twin would lose its sockets. Wear them with `import` instead.
- **The keypad, fusebox and breaker:** boxes with printed panels. Possible, but the kit's plates and panels already
  draw them.

## 8. Adding to it

- **A family** is `families/<name>.py` with `make(spec, th)`, registered in `families/__init__.py`. In order:
  1. Declare materials (`th.material`) and islands (`th.island`, sizes in mm), then `th.pack()`.
  2. Build `th.low` (and `th.high` if it needs a bake) with `mesh.py`, and deform both with the same calls.
  3. Set `th.pose` and `th.paint = fn(frames)`.
- **A design** is a function `(frame, fields, rng)` in `designs.py`, added to `DESIGNS`.
- **A finish** goes in `finish.py`.
- Then a recipe in presets.json, `propgen.py make <it>`, and `propgen.py check` (add a case to `check.CASES` for a
  new family).
