# The prop generator: small props from Python in Blender

`python tools/propgen.py` makes small props the way AssetsFactory's *Free Street Litter Sampler* says its were: "All
meshes and textures are generated by our own Python scripts in Blender. No image or 3D generator was used." A recipe
(a few lines of JSON) becomes a game-ready prop: a .glb for Godot, an .fbx, an .obj, a .blend, its textures (base
colour, ORM, normal) and a preview picture. The same recipe always gives the same prop; another seed gives another
one.

## 1. How the sampler was made

The itch.io page can't be reached from the cloud, so this comes from its note (quoted above) and its two pictures.
Every sign points to code, not photographs.

- **Shapes from measurements.** The pristine can, bottle and cups are perfectly round. They're turned from a profile:
  a 330 ml can is a real 66 mm by 115 mm can, with its domed base, necked shoulder and countersunk lid. The burger box
  is a tray swept round an outline. The paper is a subdivided sheet.
- **Damage by deformation, after the print.** The crushed cans keep both rims round while the middle buckles. The
  print folds exactly with the metal and paper, so the label was mapped on the undamaged shape and the mesh deformed
  afterwards. A photo scan would carry baked-in light and noisy geometry; these don't. The crumpled ball and the
  napkin look simulated (cloth-like), the cans look deformed by a function.
- **Labels drawn by code.** FIZZ, CLEARBROOK, VOLT and THE RIVERTON LEDGER are made-up brands in flat colours with
  simple patterns: stripes, dots, zigzags, bubbles, greeked small print. That's vector drawing with a font, then baked
  to textures. Probably Blender's own text objects and shader nodes baked with Cycles. We draw in numpy instead (§3),
  which is faster and the same on every machine.
- **Detail baked down.** Small game props (a few thousand triangles) with creases sharper than their mesh means a
  detailed copy baked into normal and occlusion maps.
- **One studio render.** A grey floor, a big soft light and Cycles. Our previews copy it.
- **"Written with the help of an AI coding assistant"** means the scripts were written with an assistant, not that
  an AI made the assets. That's the same arrangement as here.

**Photo to model (i2model)?** Not what they did: invented labels and perfect symmetry don't come from photos. A photo
*can* still drive a turned thing (a side view's silhouette gives the profile) or a print (your picture as the label:
`art: {"image": ...}`, §3). That's a possible later step (§9), not this tool's way.

## 2. Commands

```bash
python tools/propgen.py list                              # every recipe by family
python tools/propgen.py make soda_can_crushed             # one (or more) into Saved/Props/<name>/
python tools/propgen.py make soda_can --set art.brand=HANA --set crush.amount=0.9 --seed 7 --as hana_crushed
python tools/propgen.py all [family...]                   # every recipe, or a family's
python tools/propgen.py sheet [names...]                  # a contact sheet of the previews: Saved/Props/sheet.png
python tools/propgen.py library [names...]                # made props into the library as props/<name> (no Blender)
python tools/propgen.py godot [names...]                  # GDPROPS: imported by Godot 4.7, materials read back, pictured
python tools/propgen.py pbr                               # the PBR shelf's sets
python tools/propgen.py pbr-add Metal/brushed ~/Downloads/brushed_*.png   # a set onto the shelf
python tools/propgen.py vines [ivy ivy_real creeper creeper_autumn bare]   # seamless vine sets onto the shelf (§20)
python tools/propgen.py check                             # PROPGEN
```

- **Blender.** It looks for `--blender PATH`, then `$BLENDER`, then `blender` on the PATH (your Omarchy machine), then
  Blender's Python module (`python tools/retarget.py setup`, the cloud's way). It runs in the background, so no window
  opens.
- **Graphics card.** Cycles uses it when it finds one (OptiX on the 3090); `--cpu` turns that off.
- **Options.** `--formats glb,fbx,obj,blend` (all four by default), `--tex 2048` (the texture size), `--no-preview`,
  `--samples N` (the bakes'), `--library` (also into the library).
- **What it makes.** `Saved/Props/<name>/` holds `<name>.glb`, `.fbx`, `.obj` + `.mtl`, `.blend`,
  `<name>_<material>_albedo.png`, `_orm.png` and `_normal.png`, `<name>_preview.png`, and `meta.json` (the resolved
  recipe, size, triangles, time).
- **Time.** About 20 to 40 s a prop on the cloud's 4 CPUs (half of it the preview); much less on the 3090.
- **Blender versions.** Tested on Blender 4.2 (the cloud's module) and Blender 5.2 LTS (Python 3.13): PROPGEN ok on
  both, every format written. Only `bpy` and the numpy Blender ships are used; no add-ons beyond the bundled
  exporters.

## 3. A recipe

Recipes live in `Content/Studio/Props/presets.json`. A recipe names its family and what differs from that family's
defaults. `from` starts from another recipe, and every brand in it is made up.

```json
"soda_can_crushed": {"label": "the soda can stepped on", "from": "soda_can", "seed": 7,
   "crush": {"amount": 0.9, "twist": 12, "bend": 4, "buckle": 0.7, "wrinkle": 0.6},
   "wear": {"grime": 0.35, "dirt": 0.5, "scratches": 0.6, "dust": 0.25}}
```

Every family takes these:

| Key | What |
| --- | --- |
| `seed` | rolls the dice: dents, folds, crumples, wear, the small print |
| `tex` | texture size in pixels (1024 by default; 2048 for a close-up) |
| `art` | the print: `{"design": "<name>", ...its fields}`, or `{"image": "my_label.png"}` (your own picture over the island) |
| `wear` | 0-1 each: `grime`, `dirt` (gathers in creases from the baked occlusion), `dust` (on what faces up), `scratches`, `edges` (worn edges), `stains`, `fade` |
| `pose` | `stand`, `lie`, `auto` (the stablest face), `any` (a stable face by the seed), `upside`; `yaw` turns it, `down` picks which side lies down |
| `view` | the preview camera: `azim`, `elev`, `lens` |
| `relief`, `ao_gamma`, `sharp_angle` | fine-tuning: the print's emboss strength, the occlusion's depth, hard edges past that angle |
| `pbr` | a PBR set off the shelf over a material's own paint: `{"main": "pbr/Wood/oak"}`, or `{"main": {"set": "pbr/Wood/oak", "tile": 0.8, "rot": 0, "tint": "#c8b090", "alpha": 1, "normal": 1, "islands": ["top"]}}` (`tile` metres a repeat; `alpha` under 1 lets the paint show through; `islands` only those parts); `"*"` every material |
| `decals` | your pictures on any part: `[{"image": "Content/Studio/Props/images/logo.png", "island": "front", "x": 75, "y": 50, "w": 40, "h": 20, "rough": 0.3, "metal": 0, "alpha": 1, "height": 0.01}]`, `x` and `y` its middle in mm across and up the island (its middle if not given), `w` its width (a third of the island), `h` from the picture's shape; `fit` wraps it over the whole island instead: `"stretch"` to its size, `"width"` or `"height"` keeping the picture's shape; meta.json lists a prop's islands with their sizes (the page draws them) |

### Making your own

Four ways, from clicking to a family of your own:

0. **The page** (§13): `python tools/propgen/serve.py`, then http://127.0.0.1:8797. Every recipe is a card with its
   picture; open one, change it in a form, drop your pictures on its texture, pick PBR sets, make it (or several
   variations at once), turn it in 3D, keep it as a recipe. Everything below is what the page writes for you.
1. **One-off, from the command line:** `--set key.sub=value` changes anything in a recipe (JSON values: a number, a
   string, `null` to take something away), `--seed` rolls the dice again, `--as` names what comes out. For example:
   - `make mug_stoneware --set liquid=null --as mug_empty` (no tea at all),
   - `make mug_stoneware --set liquid.kind=green_tea --set liquid.fill=0.8 --as mug_green_tea`,
   - `make mug_stoneware --set 'liquid.color="#3a6a8a"' --as mug_blue` (any colour of your own),
   - `make rice_cooker --set 'logo={"image": "Content/Games/G/Art/logo.png", "w": 56}' --as my_cooker`.
2. **A recipe of your own** in presets.json, starting `from` one that exists and writing down only what differs:
   `"mug_green_tea": {"label": "...", "family": "ware", "from": "mug_stoneware", "liquid": {"kind": "green_tea", "fill": 0.8}}`.
   Then `propgen.py make mug_green_tea` and `library mug_green_tea` puts it where a story can place it.
3. **Your own pictures and textures:** `decals` puts a picture on any part of any prop, where you say (a logo on a can,
   a sticker on a pot); `art: {"image": "path.png"}` covers a whole print island with one (a can's label, a page);
   `logo` puts one on an appliance; `pbr` lays a PBR set off the shelf over a material. Paths are from the project
   folder, or absolute; a transparent PNG is cut out by its alpha. Different pictures and seeds make varied props
   from one recipe: `--seed` (or the page's Variations) rolls the dents, the wear, the folds and the small print
   again, and each variation can carry its own decals.

A cup that is sometimes empty and sometimes full doesn't need two props: make it with its liquid (even at `fill: 0`)
and set the fill and the colours on each placed copy in Godot (its `HSLiquidFill`: `fill` 0-1, `own_colors` off, then
`liquid_color`, `surface_color`, `foam_color`), or from a script while playing (pouring, drinking).

### Designs

A design draws a print from a few fields (`designs.py`). Each is a function on a Frame (one island in millimetres):

- **Drinks:** `soda`, `energy`, `beer`, `coffee_can` (Japanese text), `water`.
- **Cups:** `coffee_cup`, `sleeve`, `iced`.
- **Paper:** `newspaper` (page 1: masthead, headline, photo, columns; `page: 2`: classifieds), `newsprint`,
  `napkin`, `fries`, `receipt` (its `items` and `date`), `note` (`text` in `marker` or `typewriter`), `paper`.
- **Card:** `burger_box`, `kraft`, `card_plain`.
- **Wood and lacquer:** `wood_grain`, `lacquer`.
- **Glazes:** `glaze`, `sometsuke`, `speckled`, `jar_label`.
- **Talismans and charms:** `ofuda` (a paper talisman for a `sheet`: `text` brushed down the middle, `side` [right,
  left], `seal` and `seal2` stamped in vermilion, `border`, `age_tint`, `paper`, `ink`), `ema` (`part` front or back),
  `brocade` (`color`, `thread`, `pattern`, `text`, `name`). For your own designs: `designs.tate(frame, words, x,
  y_top, size, rng)` writes down a column, right to left at a newline, each character a little off as a brush leaves
  it; `designs.stamp(frame, x, y, size, chars, rng)` presses a seal, square or round, white characters cut out of
  red or red characters in a red border, the ink uneven.

Fonts are in `tools/propgen/fonts/`: OFL and Apache fonts from Google Fonts, with their licences beside them. Only the
rendered textures ship in a game, never the fonts (the OFL leaves what they draw free). Name one by its key (`anton`,
`bebas`, `archivo`, `pacifico`, `lobster`, `fraktur`, `serif`, `serif_bold`, `typewriter`, `marker`, `mono`,
`bungee`, `righteous`, `rubik`, `gothic_jp` for Japanese, `brush_jp` for a calligrapher's hand: Yuji Syuku, cut to
kana, full-width forms and JIS level 1 kanji, 3,649 characters) or by a .ttf path.

### Metal finishes (`finish.py`)

How bare metal was made and then used:

- `mirror`: polished to a mirror, a few hairline scratches.
- `polished`: bright, faint polishing marks.
- `satin`: a fine even grain.
- `brushed`: straight grain.
- `swirl`: the circular scratches of cutlery that's been washed and dried a thousand times.
- `scratched`: long random scratches (keys in a pocket).
- `hammered`: beaten dimples.
- `cast`: grainy and pitted.

On top of a finish, `age` lays `tarnish` (silver going warm and dull), `patina` (brass going brown, green in the
hollows) or `rust`. The metals: steel, silver, brass, copper, iron, nickel, gold, aluminium, chrome, bronze, or a
'#hex'.

### Wear

The wear layers go by each texel's place on the thing: its rest position (so the noise follows the material and has
no seams), its normal, and the occlusion and curvature baked from the detailed mesh. Dirt therefore settles into a
crushed can's folds, dust settles on what faces up, and scratches and worn edges show what's under the finish. What a
layer does depends on the material's kind:

- **Painted metal:** bare metal shows through.
- **Varnished wood:** the pale wood shows.
- **Card:** the fibres show.
- **Cloth:** it's rubbed lighter.

## 4. How a prop is made (`tools/propgen/`)

1. **The family builds the thing** (`families/<name>.py`).
   - First its islands: where each part's print goes, sized in millimetres and packed into the texture at one density
     (`build.Thing.pack`).
   - Then its meshes at rest: the game mesh, and a detailed one when it has detail the game mesh can't carry.
   - Shapes come from `mesh.py`:
     - `lathe`: a profile turned (cans, bottles, cups, ware).
     - `loft`: a profile swept round an outline, with holes and concave caps filled by constrained Delaunay (boxes).
     - `slab`: an outline cut from a sheet with rounded edges (cutlery, keys).
     - `sweep`: a section along a path (a mug's handle).
     - `sheet`, `extrude`.
2. **Deformers** (`deform.py`, `sim.py`) change only the vertices, from each vertex's own coordinates, so the game mesh
   and the detailed one deform alike:
   - `crush`: each ring of a wall keeps its length as the circle becomes a stadium, with buckles, twist and bend.
   - `dents`.
   - `displace`.
   - The paper simulation: position-based dynamics in numpy. The sheet can't stretch, its bends set into creases past a
     yield, its particles keep their distance, and it lies on the floor. It does random folds, a squeeze in a
     shrinking sphere, a fold along a line, a curl and a lie-down.
3. **Set down** (`deform.rest_pose`): the faces of its convex hull it can stand on (its centre of mass over the face),
   the one asked for, lowest point on the floor.
4. **Painted** (`paint.py`): shapes, patterns and text in millimetres per island, laying down colour, roughness,
   metalness, height and a normal (from a PBR set) together. Text comes from the font's own outlines through Blender's
   text objects; shapes are filled by the nonzero rule at 4 × 4 samples a pixel.
5. **Baked** (`bake.py`, Cycles): the detailed mesh's normals, ambient occlusion and curvature onto the game mesh's
   texture. A numpy rasterizer gives each texel its rest place, normal and tangent frame.
6. **Worn** (`surface.py`), its maps written (base colour, an ORM texture, an OpenGL normal map), its material made the
   way the glTF exporter carries it to Godot, exported, pictured (`stage.py`).

**Polycounts.** A game mesh keeps the shape. Sharp detail (crush folds, rounded edges, crimps, film folds, chips) lives
in its normal map, baked from a detailed copy. A crushed can is 10k triangles (the detail 84k), a spoon 2.3k (39k), a
key 1.7k (13k), a burger box 0.5k (17k). The cans and cups at 4k to 7k could go lower (`segments`) for far-away
litter.

## 5. Your own things, worn: `import`, and PBR sets

```json
"chair_worn": {"family": "import", "kit": "dining_chair", "seed": 3, "tex": 1024,
   "materials": {"*": {"kind": "wood"}, "kit_wood": {"pbr": "pbr/Wood/worn_planks", "tile": 0.45}},
   "dents": {"count": 12, "depth": 1.2, "radius": 18}, "detail": 6,
   "wear": {"edges": 0.7, "scratches": 0.6, "dirt": 0.45, "dust": 0.35, "grime": 0.3}}
```

- **The source:** `file` (a .glb, .fbx or .obj you made: furniture from your interiors), or `kit` (a kit preset, built
  by the kit's own code, `tools/kit/build.mjs`, with `kit_spec` for its numbers).
- **UVs:** `uv: auto` keeps them when they're one 0-1 layout, and makes new ones a material each (Smart UV Project)
  when they tile or are missing. The kit's meshes tile in metres, so they get new ones. `keep` or `new` forces it.
- **Materials,** by name (`*` for the rest):
  - `pbr`: a set off the shelf, laid by **triplanar projection at its real size** (`tile` metres a repeat), so it
    doesn't matter what the UVs are. Its normal map is turned into each texel's tangent frame.
  - `tint`, `color` / `rough` / `metal` (in place of what it had), `kind` (what wear does to it), `wear` (its own
    layers).
  - With none of these, a material keeps its own colour, and its own texture when its UVs are kept.

## 10. Liquid inside: level in Godot, sloshing

The soy bottle, the glass, the tokkuri, the mug and the water bottle carry a liquid that stays level however the
thing is turned, and swings when it's moved.

- **Valve's version:** in Half-Life: Alyx it's Matt Wilde's pixel shader on the bottle. Nothing is simulated inside;
  the liquid's plane is kept level in world space and a spring driven by the bottle's motion makes it slosh
  ([UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/alyx-liquid-shaders/),
  [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/counter-strike-2-comes-with-half-life-alyxs-most-needlessly-impressive-graphics-quirk/)).
- **The common game technique** (the Godot ports on godotshaders.com, Minions Art's Unity one): the container's
  inside is a closed mesh, cut by the shader at the level. Its back faces, seen through the cut, are drawn as the flat
  surface lit by the plane's normal. Below the level, the front faces are the body with a foam line where it meets the
  wall.
- **What we add, because the generator knows the inside exactly:** a fill table. It's made from 24,000 points spread
  evenly through the inside: for 24 tilts (upright to upside down) and 33 fills, the level that has that share of
  them below it. A bottle laid on its side therefore keeps its volume, not its height, and a narrow neck fills fast as
  it should.
- **What the .glb carries:**
  - A node of its own, `<name>_liquid`: the inside as a closed mesh with a plain material in the liquid's colour, so
    an engine without the shader shows it full.
  - Its glTF extras, `propgen_liquid` (Godot keeps them as the node's metadata `extras`): the kind and its colours
    (body, surface, foam), the clarity, the fill, the table and its range, and the container's axis and base in the
    mesh's own space.
- **Godot** (the look plugin, copied into every export):
  - `liquid.gdshaderinc` holds the shared shader code; `liquid.gdshader` is the opaque version (soy, coffee, blood) and
    `liquid_clear.gdshader` the see-through one (water, sake, tea).
  - `HSLiquid.attach(mesh)` wears the shader from the metadata, and `HSLiquid.auto(root)` adds an `HSLiquidFill` under
    every such mesh.
  - The game calls `auto` as it enters a level (game.gd `enter_level`), so a placed `asset props/soy_bottle` sloshes
    with nothing set up.
- **In the editor:** drop an `HSLiquidFill` under a liquid's mesh to see and change it.
  - Its settings: `fill` (-1 keeps the prop's own), the colours (`own_colors` off to use yours), `slosh`, `settle` and
    `swing`.
  - The swing is Minions Art's damped spring: the mesh's velocity and spin push it, it dies away, and it sets the
    plane's slope as an instance uniform (`wobble`), so many bottles share one material.
- **Kinds:** `water`, `soy`, `sake`, `tea`, `black_tea`, `green_tea`, `matcha`, `hojicha`, `barley_tea`, `milk_tea`,
  `coffee`, `whisky`, `medicine`, `blood`, `oil`, `broth`, `miso` and `curry` (`liquid.LIQUIDS`), or `color` for your
  own: it sets the liquid's body, and its surface and its line at the wall a little lighter, since in an opaque cup
  the surface is all you see.
- **The preview:** Cycles imitates the shader at rest (cut at the level in world height, back faces as the surface),
  so the picture shows what Godot will.
- **Checked:** GDPROPS stands each liquid prop upright with a second copy tipped 70° beside it, and checks that
  HSLiquid wears the right shader on every one (`Saved/Props/_godot/godot_liquids.png`).
- **Limits:**
  - The table assumes a turned inside (any tilt direction gives the same volume), which every container here is. A
    box-shaped tank would want a table by direction too.
  - The FBX and OBJ carry the inside as a full mesh with no shader.
  - A see-through liquid behind see-through glass sorts by object, as Godot does: fine for a bottle on a table, worth
    a look in a long row of them.
- **Not only turned things:** a sink's basin isn't turned, so its points come from its own rings (`sink._liquid`); a
  sink isn't tipped, so the table's tilts don't matter there. `broth`, `miso` and `curry` join the kinds for the pots.

## 11. Sockets, hard surfaces and shapes a lathe can't turn

- **Sockets:** a family sets `th.sockets[name] = (point, direction)`, and the .glb carries an empty node
  `socket_<name>` there, its arrow (+Y in Godot) along the direction, a child of the prop's mesh; meta.json lists them.
  They're where a flame, smoke, steam or a light goes. The names are the kit's (KIT.md §9), so the two read alike:
  `flame` (candles), `tip_1`... (incense), `spout`, `lid`, `steam`, `rim`, `handle`, `vent` (pots and kettles), `lcd`,
  `led_warm`, `led_cook`, `button` (a rice cooker), `switch`, `led`, `base` (a kettle), `drain` (sinks), `hang`
  (charms), `end_a`, `end_b` (a rope), `nail_tip` (a straw doll), `strike` (an orin).
- **Hard surfaces:** `th.weighted = <angle>` gives the game mesh normals weighted by face area (`mesh.weighted_normals`;
  per corner, from the faces round it within the angle). A big flat face then shades flat and its rounded bevel takes
  the highlight, however few faces the bevel has. That's the hard-surface trick that keeps a box's edges soft without
  the polygons. The shrine things, cookware, appliances and sinks use it; a recipe's `weighted` overrides it.
- **Rounded boxes:** `mesh.rbox(size, bevel, islands, top, bottom, side, corner=, fill=, step=)` is a slab of a rounded
  footprint. `fill` puts points inside its big faces (their normals stay true) and `step` rings up its sides.
  `mesh.rperim` gives its side band's length for the island.
- **Tubes:** `mesh.tube(path, radii, island, caps=)` is a sweep with a radius per point and closed ends (bundles of
  straw, ropes, handles, a kettle's spout). `mesh.ring_path` gives a closed circle for a binding or a loop. `mesh.spline`
  draws a smooth curve through a few points, its points only where it bends: a belly, a dome, a gooseneck.
- **Shapes a lathe can't turn:** `mesh.ring_strip(rings, runs, islands)` joins rings of points (each anticlockwise,
  all the same count) into one surface. A run maps as a `band` (round and along) or a `plan` (from above).
  `mesh.rrect_ring` gives a rounded rectangle's ring and its insets point for point; `mesh.toward` moves a ring onto
  an ellipse or a circle by angle. The kitchen sink is a ring strip from its rolled edge, across the deck, down the
  basin to the drain, and the washbasin one from its underside, over its rim and down its bowl.
- **A lathe's faces look to the right of its profile:** up the outside, a profile walking up faces out. A lid's top
  must therefore walk from its rim in to the middle, with its underside first from the middle out (cookware's lids do).
  A cap walked from the middle of its top outward faces into itself and takes `M.flip` (the bottles', the pills', the
  jar's and the crown corks'). The same rule holds for a loft and a slab: their band faces out when its profile climbs
  (`slab` walks it from the bottom up), and `loft` and `extrude` turn their loops the right way round themselves.
- **A mirror turns faces inside out:** laying a lathe on its side by swapping two axes (chopsticks, a key's shaft) or
  scaling by −1 mirrors it; `M.flip(m)` after it puts its faces back (it keeps the UVs).
- **Faces seen from outside are fronts, by measure:** Godot draws only a face's front, where Cycles' previews draw
  both sides, so an inside-out part looks right in every preview and is see-through in the game. PROPGEN casts rays
  at each case from 26 directions (`check.orientation`) and fails when more than 1.5% of what they hit is a face's back;
  double-sided materials are left out (both their sides are drawn). Thin things that fold over themselves (crumpled
  paper, a crushed cup, a bottle's label seen through the bottle) are double-sided: their two faces cross in the
  creases.

## 13. The page: recipes as pictures

`python tools/propgen/serve.py`, then http://127.0.0.1:8797 (`PROPS_PORT` for another; the launcher has it under
Pages). A wrapper over `tools/propgen.py`: what it writes is the same recipe JSON, and what it makes the same files.

- **The gallery:** every recipe a card with its picture (the preview of its last make; small JPEGs in

  that isn't a recipe (variations). *Make the unmade* makes every recipe not made yet.
- **The form** (Settings) is built from the recipe itself: colours as colour pickers, shares (wear, fill, burn...) as
  sliders, words as text, and lists for what the generator knows. Those are its designs, fonts, liquids, finishes,
  metals and poses, and each family's kinds and options, read from the family's own docstring (`"kind": "a" | "b"`),
  so a new family's options show up by themselves. Any setting can be added (+ a setting) or taken out (×); quick adds
  are a liquid, a print and wear. Recipe JSON is the whole recipe as text, editable.
- **Texture & pictures** is **the picture editor** (§17, after Figma): the tab takes the whole page, the texture in
  the middle with its cuts, its parts and pictures as layers on the left, the picked one's settings and the prop in 3D
  wearing the canvas on the right. Pictures are dragged onto it (a part, one of its own pictures, or the whole
  texture), moved, sized and turned by their handles. A PBR set off the shelf per material is there too, and each
  part's size, shape and the picture it wants. Pictures dropped in go to `Content/Studio/Props/images/`.
- **3D:** the made .glb, as Godot will read it, under a soft studio light and an environment for the metals; its
  sockets as blue markers pointing their way (drag to turn, wheel to zoom, double-click to frame, *turn* to spin).
- **Make** runs `propgen.py make` in the background, one at a time, its log under the form. It makes the recipe as
  the form has it, under the name in *Make as* (your recipe isn't changed by a make). *Variations* makes several at
  once with seeds a step apart, as `<name>_v1`, `_v2`.... *Texture* sets the texture size; *all formats* adds the
  fbx, obj and blend to the glb.
- **Save** writes the form into its recipe; **Save as new** keeps it under *Make as*'s name, written as `from` the
  recipe it started from and only what differs, so presets.json stays readable. Every save keeps a copy of
  presets.json in `Saved/Props/_history/` first (the last 50). **To the library** puts the made prop where a story
  places it (`asset props/<name>`); **Delete** takes a recipe out (not one others start from).
### Your own picture wrapped round a prop

Drag it onto the part in the picture editor (§17), or `decals: [{"image": ..., "island": "label", "fit":
"stretch"}]`. The generator's own work goes on over it: a crush or a dent bends the picture with the metal, and the
wear (scratches, dirt in the folds, dust on what faces up), the baked occlusion and the relief are laid on top.

- **Its size:** a material's parts share one texture at one density, so each part holds a set number of pixels. The
  page lists them: a 350 ml can's label is 208 × 101 mm and holds 1011 × 491 px at the default 1024 texture, so a
  picture of about 2000 × 1000 px stays sharp even made at 2048 (`tex`). More is wasted; less goes soft.
- **Its shape:** make it the part's shape (the page gives it: the can's label is 2.06 : 1). `stretch` squashes a
  picture of another shape to fit; `width` keeps its shape and centres it up and down.
- **Seamless?** Only for a part that goes round (the page says so: a can's or a bottle's label, a cup's side, a
  candle's), where the picture's left and right edges meet at the back. A label that's a picture (a logo in the
  middle, a plain ground to its ends) needs nothing. A pattern meant to run all the way round should tile left to
  right, or the back shows a line. A flat part (a box's top, a plaque's face, a page) never needs it.
- **Round flat faces** (a can's lid, a pot's bottom, any disc) show the middle of their island as a circle, so a
  picture's corners don't show there. A flat face cut from an outline (a plaque, a box's side) shows the middle nine
  tenths of its island.
- **A picture's alpha** cuts it out (a sticker, a logo on the paint), and its whole area is your colour, so wear and
  the folds' shading still darken it as they do the print.

- **Checked:** PROPPAGE (`node tools/propgen/pagetest.mjs`): a headless browser on a scratch copy of the recipe book
  and scratch folders (`PROPS_PRESETS`, `PROPS_OUT`, `PROPS_IMAGES`), so yours are never touched. It opens the ema and
  changes its words, makes it (its log, its picture on its card, the paint under your pictures beside its maps), then
  in the picture editor: its cuts and parts as layers and the prop in 3D wearing the canvas; a file dropped from the
  desktop onto the front, its shape, filling it; a logo dropped onto the back, placed where it lands; onto the front's
  motif, taking its place; the logo sized, turned and moved by its handles and Ctrl+Z; a picture the texture's shape
  over all of it in its layout; a smudge; the logo in each corner; made again, the made texture where the canvas said;
  3D with its sockets; a PBR set; the .ora template out and back in; a part picked in 3D; two variations; a recipe
  saved `from ema` with only what differs, and deleted. A few minutes here; Blender makes four props.

## 14. Enhancers in Godot: what glows, burns, smokes and steams

A made prop can bring a little more than its maps: LEDs and screens that glow, candle flames with their light,
incense smoke, steam, wax the light shines into. The generator says which a prop has; the look plugin's `HSPropFX`
does them in Godot, each a switch.

- **In the glb:**
  - What glows is painted as an emission map (the painter's `emit`): the rice cooker's LCD backlight (its lit
    segments dark on it) and its cook and warm lights, the kettle's switch light, the incense's burning rings, a
    candle's wick tip. It's the material's emissive texture with its strength (`KHR_materials_emissive_strength`), so
    any glTF viewer shows it glowing.
  - The list of enhancers in the extras (`propgen_fx`): `glow` (the glowing materials, their energy, what they
    follow: a candle's ember goes out with its flame), `flame`, `smoke`, `steam` (the sockets each starts from, and
    whether it's `on` at first), `wax` (the materials).
- **Which props:**

  | Prop | Enhancers |
  | --- | --- |
  | candles | flame at every wick (lit once `burn` is over 0), glow (the ember), wax |
  | kōro | smoke from every incense tip, glow (the embers) |
  | rice cookers | glow (LCD, lights), steam at the vent (off at first) |
  | electric kettles | glow (the switch light), steam at the spout (off at first) |
  | pots and pans | steam from an open pot, the donabe's vent, the tetsubin's spout (off at first) |

- **A recipe's own:** `fx` changes them: `"fx": {"steam": {"on": true}}` (a pot on the boil), `"fx": {"flame": false}`
  (no flame at all), `"fx": {"glow": {"energy": 8}}`.
- **Godot-ready:** every make writes `<name>.tscn` beside the glb: the prop with its `HSPropFX` (and its liquid's
  `HSLiquidFill`) on it. The glb is named relative to the scene, so copy the two together into any project with the
  look plugin, drop the scene in a level, and switch its enhancers in the inspector.
- **In the game:** the game puts an `HSPropFX` on every made prop as it enters a level (`HSPropFX.auto`, beside
  `HSLiquid.auto`).
- **The switches** (the node's inspector, or a script while playing: a pot coming to the boil):
  - `enabled`;
  - `glow`, `glow_energy` (−1 the prop's own), `glow_flicker` (a dying screen);
  - `flame`, `flame_light`, `flame_shadows`, `flame_size`, `flame_color`;
  - `smoke`, `steam`, `puff_rate`;
  - `wax`, `wax_glow`.
  
  A kind off for the whole project: Project Settings, `horrorstudio/prop_fx/glow` (`flame`, `smoke`, `steam`,
  `wax`); `horrorstudio/prop_fx/enabled` off and the game puts none on.
- **What they are:**
  - Glow: the prop's own emission, by a per-copy material override (turned up, down, off, flickering).
  - Flame: a teardrop drawn by `flame.gdshader` on a quad turning round the world's up, swaying and breathing by
    noise, added over the scene; an `HSFlickerLight` in candle mode a little above it, with shadows.
  - Smoke and steam: soft puffs (`puff.gdshader`, as the little things' in lifefx.gd) born at the socket, rising,
    curling, growing and fading.
  - Wax: subsurface scattering and backlight on the wax, so a lit candle glows into its wax.
  
  None of them is saved into your level: what HSPropFX makes has no owner, and the prop's materials go back as they
  were before every save.
- **Checked:**
  - PROPGEN: the enhancers in each case's glb, an emission map where it glows, its `.tscn` written.
  - GDPROPS: every prop with enhancers in the dark, given its HSPropFX as the game gives it, steam switched on. Each
    enhancer is measured lit (what glows, the flames and their lights, the puffs, the wax), then every switch off (all
    gone), and each prop's `.tscn` is loaded with its HSPropFX. Pictured in `Saved/Props/_godot/godot_fx.png`.

## 15. Your pictures on any prop: its own, decals, flourishes, smudges

Every prop takes your pictures four ways, all on the page's Texture & pictures tab and in the recipe as plain JSON.
Each goes on the paint before the bake and the wear, so the folds, dents, baked detail, grime and scratches go over it.

- **Its own pictures (slots).** Whatever a prop's paint draws that you'd want to swap is a slot: a brand name or logo,
  a picture (the soda's orange slice, the torii on an ema), an ornament or border, a stamp or seal, a print, a
  pattern. Each is `<island>.<name>` (`label.logo`, `label.fruit`, `front.seal` on an ofuda, `front.motif` on an
  ema...), listed in meta.json's `slots` with its box (mm on its island), its kind (logo, picture, flourish, stamp,
  text, pattern) and what it is: 116 on 50 of the recipes, docs/PROPGEN_PICTURES.md lists every prop's. A recipe swaps
  them in `pictures`: `{"label.logo": "Content/Studio/Props/images/my_logo.png"}`, or `{"image": ..., "fit": "contain"
  | "cover" | "stretch" | "tile", "tile" (mm), "scale", "dx", "dy" (mm), "rot", "flip", "tint", "rough", "metal",
  "height", "relief"}`, or
  `false` for nothing there. A key with `*` swaps every slot it matches: `"*.logo"` the twelve cigarette butts' brand
  at once, `"*.pattern"` every printed part of a burger box. Your picture takes the drawn one's place, kept to its own
  shape inside the box (`contain`) unless you say otherwise; `tint` recolours a one-colour logo. A pattern, or a strip
  of ornament four times as wide as it's high, repeats (`tile`): `tile` mm a repeat's width, else a strip's picture at
  the strip's height and anything else at its own size (two of its pixels a texel, as the sizes are reckoned), and
  round a part that goes round, a whole number of repeats so they meet at its seam. So a pattern can be the whole area
  at the size it wants or a small tile seamless all round. On a part that goes round (a can's or a bottle's label, a
  cup's band), a picture, a decal or a smudge that crosses its seam carries on from the other side. A stamped or moulded slot (a key's or a knife's mark, a can lid's words, a cap's moulding) takes only your
  picture's shape, pressed in or raised: where it's opaque, or where it's dark if it has no transparency. Swapping one
  slot leaves the rest of the paint as it was. A design or a family adds a slot by drawing through
  `f.slot(name, (cx, cy, w, h), draw, kind, about, **ink)` (paint.py; `relief=True` for a stamp): the drawing runs
  only when nothing is swapped in; designs.py's `text_box`, `union` and `on` measure a box from what's drawn.
- **Decals and flourishes.** `decals` (§13) put a picture anywhere on any part. A flourish is a decal repeated:
  `repeat: n` spreads n copies along the part (`along` x or y, `gap` mm between them, `mirror` every other one
  flipped), `corners: true` puts one in each corner `inset` mm in, each mirrored to face its corner (draw it for the
  top left); `flip` mirrors one, `tint` recolours it.
- **Smudges.** `smudges`: [{image, island (or "*": any part, by its area), x, y (its middle, mm), w (mm), rot,
  strength, blend, tint, rough, height, count, seed}]. `multiply` darkens toward the smudge's colour (coffee, soot,
  grease, blood); `mix` lays its colour (dust, water marks, rust). `rough` is the roughness it leaves (grease shiny,
  dust matt), `height` mm it stands proud, `count` that many scattered at random places, turns and sizes. Nine are
  built in, drawn by code (smudges.py) into `tools/propgen/smudges/` the first time they're used (not kept in git):
  `smudge/coffee_ring`, `fingerprint`, `grease`, `drip`, `soot`, `water_mark`, `blood`, `dust`, `rust`. Your own: a
  PNG transparent round it (its colour where it's opaque), or a picture dark on white (no alpha: dark is the stain).
- **Wrapping a whole part** (§13): your own label over all of it.

**What size a picture wants.** The texture's pixels a millimetre at that place, twice over, rounded up to 16 (never
under 64): sharp in a close-up and on a texture twice the size. Bigger is fine (it's scaled down); smaller goes soft.
The page shows the size on every own picture, part, decal and smudge (hover for the millimetres and the density),
and `python tools/propgen.py pictures` writes every made prop's own pictures and parts with theirs into
docs/PROPGEN_PICTURES.md. Logos, flourishes, stamps and text want a PNG transparent round them. A picture over a part
that goes all the way round (a can's or a bottle's label, a cup's band, a candle) is seamless left to right: its left
and right edges meet at the back.

**Templates.** Each own picture's *template* on the page downloads the generator's own drawing there, cropped from the
made texture at the size it wants: paint over it (Krita, anything) and swap it back in.

**On the page:** the picture editor (§17): its own pictures are layers under each part (a crop of each, or yours) and
dashed boxes on the canvas, a picture dropped on one takes its place; its settings swap it, turn it off, fit, scale,
move and tint it and give its template. Pictures and flourishes, smudges and their settings likewise. Checked by
PROPPAGE.

## 18. Trees: a street's trees grown by Mtree

The user (2026-11-02): "Similar to our prop system can you see if you can incorporate this blender library ... and
create a tree prop generator, trees don't need to be massive, just stuff that would be in urban streets, make some
variations", pointing at steven-ray's fork of Maxime Herpin's **Modular Tree** (`modular_tree`, the Blender 3.x build
of Mtree 4). Mtree 4 is a C++ core (MIT) with a node-based Blender add-on (GPL) over it; the add-on has to be compiled
against each Blender's Python, and the fork stopped at 3.2. So the core's growth is ported, not the add-on:
`tools/propgen/mtree.py` is Mtree's trunk, branch and pipe-radius functions in plain numpy Python (the same tree in
the same order of steps, under the same words: start, end, density, length, start angle, randomness, up attraction,
flatness, gravity, stiffness, split chance and angle, break chance, phyllotaxis, the pipe model's power), and it runs
inside Blender 4.2's and 5.x's own Python with nothing to build. Mtree's meshers (the manifold one in C++, the add-on's
leaves by a geometry-nodes modifier) are not ported: the tree family meshes the tree for a game its own way, below.
Nothing of modular_tree's code is copied in; the Mtree core's licence (MIT) and the lineage are in `mtree.py`'s own
words.

**The family** (`families/tree.py`, `"family": "tree"`):

- **Grown**: `kind` picks a species (its numbers are the species' at its default height, scaled to `height`): the
  trunk (`clear` metres bare below the first branch, as a street tree is pruned), two or three orders of branches
  each on the one before, the pipe model's radii (a trunk never thinner than its species' says, however few tips
  feed it), the last order carrying the leaves. The knobs over the species: `spread` turns the branches out, `droop`
  is gravity on them, `density` how many, `crown` their length, `levels` how many orders, `lean` degrees off the
  vertical, `multi` stems from the ground (a crape myrtle's clump), `pollard` (a plane tree cut back to knuckles
  with a broom of shoots), `twigs` (`auto`: the last order is meshed only when the tree is bare; `mesh`; `cards`),
  `twigs_density`, `radius` (the trunk's at the ground). The same seed grows the same tree.
- **Meshed**: every branch a tube with sides by its thickness (three on a twig, twelve on a trunk), rooted inside its
  parent with a collar, the stems flared at the root with lobes; closed at the tip. Its UVs go round the tube and up
  it in circumferences, so one square tile of bark stays square however thick the branch. The leaves are **cards**
  on the last order's nodes and at every branch's tip: a card stands along its twig and out of the crown, drooping a
  little, and carries one of four clusters off the leaf atlas; its vertex normals are rounded over the crown (out of
  the crown's middle, lifted) so the mass lights as one, not as flat cards; `leaves.count` how many (the species'
  own), `leaves.size` metres a card. A street tree is 9k to 26k triangles leafy (a camphor's dome the most), a bare one with its twigs 20k to
  30k; a sapling 2k.
- **The bark is a real one by default** (the user, 2026-11-02: "every tree should use the more real bark pbr"): each
  species names a bark set off the shelf (`bark.pbr`: the user's ambientCG sets in `pbr/bark/`, Bark001 grey and
  deeply furrowed for the ginkgo, the camphor and the dead tree, Bark006 grey-blue mottled with orange flakes for the
  zelkova, the plane, the maple and the crape myrtle, Bark014 red-brown fissured plates for the pine and the cherry;
  `bark.tint` over it, `bark.tile` metres a repeat, `bark.relief` the normal's strength). The set is laid over the
  whole bark tile with a whole number of repeats each way (its own size: a 1 x 2 m set repeats half as often up), so
  it still goes round and up every branch without a seam. `"bark": {"pbr": null}` draws the tile by code instead:
- **Painted by code**, the rest of it, and the drawn **bark** when asked: one tile over the whole of its texture, drawn
  on the flat torus with 4D noise (`noise.fbm4`, `ridged4`: it repeats round and up with no seam and nothing
  squeezed) in the species' style: `furrowed` (ginkgo, camphor, the dead tree: ridges up the trunk, dark cracks between), `flaky` (zelkova:
  grey flaking to orange-brown patches with a dark rim), `lenticels` (cherry: dark bark banded with pale lenticels,
  a few cracks), `mottled` (plane, crape myrtle: plates of three colours), `scaly` (pine: a jigsaw of plates parted by
  deep fissures), `smooth` (maple); `bark.color`, `bark.relief`, `bark.scale`; or a PBR set off the shelf
  (`"pbr": {"bark": "pbr/<set>"}`, tiled at its own size). The **leaf atlas** is four clusters: a twig with side
  twigs and the species' leaf (elliptic, ovate, a ginkgo's fan, a maple's or a plane's lobes, a pine's tuft of
  needles) hung thick along them in the season's colours (`season`: summer, spring's lighter greens, autumn's; a
  cherry in spring and a crape myrtle in summer carry flowers instead), see-through everywhere else. The leaf
  material is cut, not blended (the glTF's alphaMode MASK: Godot's alpha scissor, so leaves cast shadows of their
  own shape and need no sorting) and double-sided. `leaves.tint` tints a season's colours.
- **A street tree's props**: `stakes` (`torii`: two posts and a crossbar bound to the trunk with black bands, the
  Japanese 二脚鳥居型; `tripod`: three poles leaning on it, bound; `single`: a post beside it), `pit` (`kerb`: a
  square concrete kerb round dark soil, 1.2 m, `pit_size`; `soil`: the square of soil alone).
- **Set down by its trunk**: a tree's origin is the foot of its trunk, not the middle of its bounds (`Thing.anchor`,
  meta.json's `anchor`), so a placed tree stands where it's put and its crown leans where it leans; `socket_base`
  there and `socket_crown` at the crown's middle. No occlusion is baked (`Thing.bake_ao` off: the bark tile covers
  every branch, a bake over it would be a jumble) and no wear is laid on the bark or the leaves (their paint carries
  the age); `wear` still reaches the stakes and the pit.

**Realistic leaves, the second category** (the user, 2026-11-02: "for leaves create a more realistic category where
you use patterns made from the pbrs I shared with you for the leaves"): the user's leaf sets (ambientCG's LeafSet005
