## The pitch

16 August, the last day of Obon, in a river town in Iwate. Haruki Onodera, thirty, hasn't let anyone into his late
mother's apartment in months. It's her first Obon since she died, and it's twelve years to the day since Natsumi
Kikuchi, his childhood friend and first love, vanished on the night of the river festival. And tonight Natsumi is
coming to visit. He has twenty minutes to tidy up.

She arrives in a summer storm, soaked and shivering, and she remembers almost everything. Her lilac eyes are just as
he remembers them. Almost. As the fireworks go up and the lanterns for the dead float down the river, small things
stop adding up: the tea she says is perfect, the hand she eats with, the missing woman on the news who looks just
like her, the police officer who calls Haruki "the boy from back then". Is she a ghost come home for Obon? Is
something wearing her face? Or is something wrong with him? And why do his own answers keep giving him away?

## What kind of game

- First person, one night, one apartment (a 2LDK) with its balcony and corridor. About 75 minutes on a first
  playthrough: her lines type out with a blip and can't be skipped. Played again for the other endings and the
  secrets.
- Chilla's Art's gameplay: a to-do list, things you pick up, open, wipe, carry and look at; her requests (she asks,
  waits, then carries on). No chases, no quick-time events, no game overs: bad things happen in endings.
- L.A. Noire's talks: close-ups where her face answers before her words. You answer with an act and a thought, never
  a speech: `[ Greet ] She's probably cold. I should let her inside.`
- **Two mysteries at once:** who she is (read from her face), and who you are (read from your own answers, thoughts
  and hands).
- Three endings (the intended one, the other way it could have gone, a secret one), secrets that rejoin the story,
  and about two dozen achievements.
- English text first, simple enough to swap for Japanese through the string tables later. No voices.

## The place and the day

- **Kirisawa** (fictional), a small river town in Iwate, Tohoku. Below the bridge, a low weir of stones crosses the
  river; kids used to run across it to watch the fireworks from the sandbank. It was forbidden.
- **16 August 2026**, the last day of Obon, when families send the dead back with a fire at the door (*okuribi*) and
  paper lanterns down the river (*tōrō nagashi*). Natsumi vanished on this night in 2014. It's also the Fire Horse
  year (research §5.7). From 17:30, in the heat and the cicadas, to the first light just before five.
- **The weather:** an evening storm (*yūdachi*) breaks as she arrives, passes before the fireworks, and comes back in
  the small hours to take the power.
- **The apartment**: unit 302, third floor of a 1990s block. A 2LDK:

The names hide the four seasons: Haru (spring), Natsu (summer), Aki (autumn), Fuyu (winter). The sisters' mother used

## Her eyes

Natsumi's eyes were a rare pale lilac, a darker ring round the iris and flecks in it, the thing everyone remembered.
Fuyumi's are brown, so tonight she wears lilac coloured contacts (common in Japan; research §5b). They're nearly right
and too even: no ring, no flecks, the same flat lilac in every light. She rubs them; there are eye drops in her bag.
After her bath she comes out with **one lilac eye and one brown**: a lens lost in the water. She turns away, says
it's the light, and comes back with both lilac again. The player has seen the album photo, so they know what
Natsumi's eyes looked like. Ghost reading: her eye changed colour. Ordinary reading: a contact lens. Both are on the
screen at once. (The Character Studio's eye already does irises in any colour and pattern: both pairs of eyes are
ours to make.)

## Two mysteries: her, and you

### Clues about her (read from her face)
The tea she says is perfect, her right hand, the negi she pushes aside, the eyes, what she knows and what she gets
wrong, what she hides from the officer, what's in her bag, "Haru-nii".

### Clues about you (read from your own answers, thoughts and hands)
The player starts out investigating her, and slowly notices the evidence is about him.
- **The answers you're offered tell on you.** Some choices only someone in his state would think of, and just seeing
  them is a clue: `[ Lock it ] Twice. Then check it again.`, `[ Turn off the TV ] Before it says my name.`,
  `[ Hide my hands ] She doesn't need to see them.`
- **Her questions are probes, and the true answers expose him.** "Do you still go down to the river?" `[ Lie ] I
  don't go out.` / `[ Truth ] Every year. Today.` Answering honestly costs him, and earns her trust.
- **Thoughts that correct themselves.** Now and then the inner monologue types out a line, stops, backs up and
  rewrites it: *"I walked her home that night."* becomes *"I walked home that night."* His memory is being edited in
  front of you.
- **What your hands show.** When he reaches for a cup or a door, his palms are scarred (from the weir's stones). The
  inner monologue never mentions it; the player sees it.
- **What you find in your own home.** Notes on the fridge in his handwriting ("Don't answer the phone after eleven";
  "Pills: did you?"). The taped closet. Her letters to him that aren't hers. And on his mother's Obon altar, two
  cucumber horses: one for his mother, and one he made for Natsumi. Part of him has always known she's dead.
- **What you don't know.** Ask her something and her face shows you got it wrong: "You said I left without saying
  goodbye. Haru-kun, I didn't leave. You know that."

Every clue about him is a **memory** (a flag). The more he has, the more of the truth he can tell at the end; with
few, his confession is partial, and her face shows she knows it.

## What the player believes, in order (the believability ladder)

Each strange thing arrives with an ordinary explanation first.

1. She looks a little different. → Twelve years, make-up; he's remembering her wrong.
2. A black-bordered letter in his unopened mail: Natsumi was declared dead this spring, and her family invites him to
   her first Obon. → A mistake? Then who phoned from the station? It's Obon: the dead come home. *(the first ghost
   reading)*
3. She's soaked and shivering in August, and asks for something hot. She says the tea is perfect, and her mouth
   tightens; later her cup is empty, and the tea is in the sink. → The storm; she's being polite. → She lies about
   little things. Why?
4. She eats right-handed; Natsumi was left-handed. Her lilac eyes have no ring. After the bath, one eye is brown. →
   He's remembering wrong; a contact lens. → Something is wearing her face *(the second ghost reading)*.
5. The missing woman on the news looks like her. She changes the channel and hides from the officer. → She owes
   money, she says. → She's mixed up in something *(she's dangerous)*.
6. The officer calls Haruki "the Onodera boy, from back then. Twelve years today." → Everyone was questioned. → What
   happened back then? *(the first time the player suspects Haruki)*
7. His hands, his notes, the second cucumber horse, the pills in the bathroom, the nurse's messages, the wall in his
   closet. → He's ill, and the strange things are in his head *(the Shutter Island reading)*.
8. On the balcony, watching the lanterns, she says "Haru-nii, look." Only one person ever called him that: Natsumi's
   little sister. → She's Fuyumi *(the truth about her)*.
9. The old tape and the diary. → What his mother did *(the truth about him)*.
10. And yet, even with his head clear: a call that says "moshi" only once, small wet footprints, a lantern floating
    upstream *(what's left over: the secret)*.

## What really happened (for us, not the player)

- **16 August 2014.** Haruki and Natsumi, eighteen, watch the fireworks and float a lantern for her grandmother. She
  tells him she's leaving for Tokyo in the spring; he tells her he loves her. She laughs, not unkindly (only the
  secret ending lets us hear what she actually said). He sulks. Walking home, the strap of her geta snaps (a bad omen,
  everyone knows), and she leaves the sandal on the bank and runs barefoot onto the weir, the forbidden shortcut, the
  river high from the afternoon's storm: "Race you!" She slips on the stones. He goes into the water after her, tears
  his hands on the weir, reaches, can't reach her. The current takes her at 23:47.
- He runs home soaked, his hands bleeding, carrying her geta. His mother decides in a minute: she cleans his hands,
  washes his clothes, and when the Kikuchis phone at two in the morning she tells them Haruki was home by eleven and
  that he and Natsumi said goodbye at the bridge. The search follows the road from the bridge; the swollen river
  keeps her. Haruki says what his mother told him to say, and in time he believes it. He keeps the geta.
- **At 22 the illness comes.** In his mind Natsumi is alive in Tokyo, and the girl on the posters isn't her. For years
  he writes to her at her parents' address: love, apologies ("I should have held on"), delusions ("they replaced
  you").
- **Last winter** his mother dies in the bath. **This spring** the Kikuchis have Natsumi declared dead and bury an empty
  coffin. Fuyumi reads his letters, which her parents never opened, and decides he knows something.
- **In July** she sends him a summer greeting card in her sister's handwriting: "I'm coming home for Obon. Can I visit
  you on the 16th? Natsu." On the 12th she leaves Tokyo with a note and without her phone, and her family reports her
  missing. Tonight, on the anniversary, she plays her sister, the one his letters write to, to make him remember.
  It's Shutter Island's role-play, staged by one grieving woman.
- **Two things are true at once:** Haruki is ill, and Natsumi has never quite left. It's Obon, when the dead come
  home. Everything strange tonight is his illness, except a handful of things that happen even with his head clear.

## How it plays

### Tasks, requests and the hidden clean-up
The to-do list carries the night. The first task is hidden and timed: tidy up before she arrives (about two minutes
from her call; the player isn't told). Take out the rubbish, wash the dishes, wipe the table, take in the laundry
before the storm, set out his mother's Obon altar, put your things away. Whatever's left, she sees and comments on. If
the pill organiser is still on the table she sees that too, and the evening changes: she's more careful, and his
illness comes out sooner. After that come her requests: a towel, something hot to drink, somen for dinner, the bath,
the futon, and later more tea. She asks, waits where she is, and the scene carries on once it's done.

### Talks: reading her face (L.A. Noire, turned round)
- The intimate moments are close-ups: at the low table, on the balcony, at the door. She speaks; her face (a mocapped
  expression) answers before her words; you answer with an act and a thought.
- Answers come in three kinds. **Trust her** (`[ Greet ]`, `[ Let it go ]`). **Look closer** (`[ Question ]`,
  `[ Look ]`): she reacts, and a tell may show. **Accuse** (`[ Accuse ] You're not Natsu.`): offered only when you hold
  evidence about her, as in L.A. Noire.
- **The turn:** she's reading your face too. Every question she asks about the past is part of her interrogation, and
  each time you repeat the story about the bridge, her face tells you she knows it's a lie.
- **The narrator you can't trust:** Haruki's inner monologue tells you what her face means. Off his medication it
  reads anger into fear and lies into grief ("She's smirking"), which is how paranoia reads faces (research §7.2),
  while the performance itself shows a frightened woman. The player learns to trust what they see over what they're
  told.

### The pills
His medication is in the bathroom cabinet, where he hid it while tidying. He can take it after dinner, around
midnight, and when she wakes at two. Each dose quiets the hallucinations and the paranoia in his inner monologue;
none is required. The game never preaches, but it shows him: with his head clear the night gets quieter, and what's
left is real. At two in the morning the same pill can go into his mouth or into her tea.

### What's tracked
- **Karma** (her trust): honest and kind answers raise it; lies, snooping she catches and cruelty lower it. It decides
  how much she lets him see, whether she tells him who she is, and how each ending plays.
- **Evidence about her** (flags): the tea, the hand, the negi, the eyes, "Haru-nii", the photo the officer shows, her
  bag, her call. Three or more unlock `[ Accuse ]`.
- **Memories about him** (flags): the scars noticed, the notes, the second cucumber horse, the true answers given, the
  corrections seen, the wall, the tape, the diary. They decide how much of the truth he can tell at the end.
- **Signs** (flags): what happens even with his head clear (the single "moshi", the wet footprints, the lantern going
  upstream, the bell at the altar, a second voice in the song).
- **Doses** (a count, 0 to 3).

## The three endings

**1. The intended ending: First Light.** He tells the truth: the weir, her geta on the bank, going in after her,
running home, the story about the bridge his mother gave him. He gives Fuyumi the geta. Her face goes through
everything (rage, grief, then something like relief): "Twelve years. Someone finally said it." She doesn't forgive
him, but she stays. In the first light, as the higurashi cicadas start up, they watch the mist on the
river where her sister is. His pill organiser is on the table where she can see it. At seven the doorbell rings, and
he opens the door to Officer Sasaki himself. *He was the villain of the past: his silence kept her family waiting
twelve years.*
- How: the truth told at the end (`[ Remember ]`), her cup never touched. With few memories his confession is partial,
  and she leaves at dawn still not knowing everything.

**2. The other way it could have gone: Kamikakushi** (神隠し, *spirited away*). He never lets himself see her.
Untreated, afraid, sure something is wearing Natsumi's face (it is, just not the way he thinks), he keeps to the
bridge story, and when she goes for the door he stops her. Cut to black on her face, one lilac eye and one brown.
Morning: the apartment is spotless, and he washes two cups. The doorbell: Officer Sasaki. "A neighbour saw a young
woman come up here last night." "She left in the night." (The bridge story again.) In the corridor a new poster goes
up beside the faded one: *Fuyumi Kikuchi, 28, missing since 16 August.* His inner monologue: "Natsumi's coming next
summer. I should keep the place clean." *He was the villain, then and now.* Nothing is shown.
- How: the pill in her cup, or `[ Stop her ]` at the door. (A `[ Deny ]` at the end isn't final: she goes for the
  door, and he chooses between calling her back with the truth and stopping her.)

**3. The secret ending: Shiki** (四季 / 死期). Everything in First Light, and more. With his head clear and his mother's
diary open between them, Fuyumi reads what really happened: he went into the river after Natsumi, his hands bled from
the weir, and his mother lied. Then the phone rings. "Moshi." He answers "moshi moshi", and a girl's voice gives the
call they used in hide-and-seek: "Mō ii yo" ("ready: come and find me"). Fuyumi hears it too, the first time anyone
has heard what he hears. The balcony door is open, and small wet footprints lead to the railing. In the first light,
caught on the weir's stones, a red geta strap: the sandal's other half. For a moment, in the glass behind Fuyumi, her
sister stands there, eighteen, in her red yukata, smiling. Summer and winter in the same room. Then, late, the two of
them light the sending-off fire at the door that nobody lit twelve years ago, for Natsumi and for his mother. *He
wasn't the villain; the illness and the lie were real, and so is she.*
- How: everything First Light needs, plus at least two doses, the 23:47 call answered "moshi moshi", and the diary
  read (the wet footprints lead to the drawer).

## The lines

Her lines are short: a beat each, about twelve English words, one take, plain English that will translate cleanly.
Childhood lives in the details (the river, the fireflies, the festival, the sandbank, ramune bottles, the summer they
were ten), not in speeches. Some of hers:

- "Haru-kun. You got tall."
- "I'm freezing. Isn't that silly, in August?"
- "You cleaned. For me?"
- "Perfect. Just how I like it."
- "Do you still go down to the river?"
- "You used to say my eyes were the colour of the morning glories."
- "Mom used to say summer and winter can't be in the same room."
- "Haru-nii, look."
- "What happened on the weir?"
- "My name is Fuyumi. I knew you'd open the door for her."

His are acts and thoughts. Some read her; some give *him* away:

- `[ Greet ] She's probably cold. I should let her inside.` / `[ Question ] Did she always look like this?`
- `[ Two sugars ] She always took two.` / `[ Ask ] Maybe she takes it differently now.`
- `[ Question ] Were her eyes always that flat?` / `[ Let it go ] Twelve years. I'm remembering it wrong.`
- `[ Truth ] Every year. Today.` / `[ Lie ] I don't go out.`
- `[ Lock it ] Twice. Then check it again.` / `[ Hide my hands ] She doesn't need to see them.`
- `[ Remember ] We walked home over the weir.` / `[ Deny ] We said goodbye at the bridge.`
- `[ Accuse ] You're not Natsu.`

## Faces (MiniMax post prompts, 4 seconds)

About fifty faces for the talks, in the writers' room's form: what the face visibly does, no words. For example:

- *the sugar*: she sips, her mouth tightens for a moment, then she smiles too widely at the viewer.
- *the lens*: she blinks hard twice, her fingers go to the corner of one eye, then she smiles and looks away.
- *the door*: her eyes flick past the viewer to the door, come back, and she smiles.
- *Haru-nii*: she freezes, her lips part, her eyes fill and she blinks it away, then holds the viewer's gaze.
- *the salt*: she laughs with her whole face, then catches herself and covers her mouth.
- *the bridge*: her face goes still and hard; she breathes out slowly through her nose and looks down.
- *your hands*: her eyes drop to the viewer's hands, stay there a moment too long, then come back up, careful.
