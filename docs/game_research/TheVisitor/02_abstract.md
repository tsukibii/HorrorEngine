# The Visitor (来訪者): the abstract (step 2)

> Waiting for the user's yes. Nothing is charted or built until then. The research behind it: `01_research.md`.

## The pitch

New Year's Eve in a snowbound river town in Iwate. Haruki Onodera, thirty, hasn't let anyone into his late mother's
apartment in months. Tonight Natsumi Kikuchi is coming: his childhood friend and first love, who left for Tokyo twelve
years ago. He has twenty minutes to tidy up. She arrives cold and smiling, and she remembers almost everything.

As the temple bells ring across the frozen river, small things stop adding up: the tea she says is perfect, the mole
on the wrong cheek, the woman on the news who looks just like her, the police officer who calls Haruki "the boy from
back then". Is she a ghost? Is something wearing her face? Or is something wrong with him? The pills in his bathroom
cabinet say one thing; her face says another. The truth is under the ice.

## What kind of game

- First person, one night, one apartment (a 2LDK) with its balcony and corridor. About 75 minutes on a first
  playthrough: her lines type out with a blip and can't be skipped. Played again for the other endings and the
  secrets.
- Chilla's Art's gameplay: a to-do list, things you pick up, open, wipe, carry and look at; her requests (she asks,
  waits, then carries on). No chases, no quick-time events, no game overs: bad things happen in endings.
- L.A. Noire's talks: close-ups where her face answers before her words. You answer with an act and a thought, never
  a speech: `[ Greet ] She's probably cold. I should let her in.`
- Three endings (the intended one, the other way it could have gone, a secret one), secrets that rejoin the story,
  and about two dozen achievements.
- English text first, simple enough to swap for Japanese through the string tables later. No voices.

  whose bells ring the old year out. In winter the shallows freeze, and kids used to cut across the ice. It was
  forbidden.
- **31 December**, the last night of a Fire Horse year (research §5.7), from 17:40 to the first sunrise, around seven.
- **The apartment**: unit 302, third floor of a 1990s block. A 2LDK:

The names hide the four seasons: Haru (spring), Natsu (summer), Aki (autumn), Fuyu (winter). The sisters' mother used
to say summer and winter could never be in the same room. In Japanese, *shiki* means both 四季, "the four seasons",
and 死期, "the hour of one's death": the secret ending's title.

## What the player believes, in order (the believability ladder)

Each strange thing arrives with an ordinary explanation first.

1. She looks a little different. → Twelve years, make-up; he's remembering her wrong.
2. A mourning postcard in his mail says she was declared dead this year. → A mistake? Then who phoned from the
   station? *(the first ghost reading)*
3. She says the tea is perfect, and her mouth tightens; later her cup is empty, and the tea is in the sink. → Too
   sweet; she's being polite. → She lies about little things. Why?
4. She eats right-handed; Natsumi was left-handed. Her mole is under the wrong eye. → He's remembering wrong. → She's
   a reflection, something from the other side of the mirror *(the second ghost reading)*.
5. The missing woman on the news looks like her. She changes the channel and hides from the officer. → She owes
   money, she says. → She's mixed up in something *(she's dangerous)*.
6. The officer calls Haruki "the Onodera boy, from back then". → Everyone was questioned. → What happened back then?
   *(the first time the player suspects Haruki)*
7. The pills in the bathroom, the nurse's messages, the wall in his closet. → He's ill, and the strange things are in
   his head *(the Shutter Island reading)*.
8. At midnight she says "Happy New Year, Haru-nii". Only one person ever called him that: Natsumi's little sister.
   → She's Fuyumi *(the truth about her)*.
9. The old tape and the diary. → What his mother did *(the truth about him)*.
10. And yet, even with his head clear: a call that says "moshi" only once, small wet footprints, a 109th bell *(what's
    left over: the secret)*.

## What really happened (for us, not the player)

- **31 December 2014.** Haruki and Natsumi, eighteen, go to the temple across the river for the bells. She tells him
  she's leaving for Tokyo in March; he tells her he loves her. She laughs, not unkindly (only the secret ending lets us
  hear what she actually said). He sulks. Walking home after the 108th bell she runs out onto the river ice, the
  forbidden shortcut: "Race you!" He grabs for her and gets only her red scarf. The ice breaks. He crawls out to her,
  reaches, can't reach her. She goes under at 00:13.
- He runs home soaked to the waist, his hands cut by the ice. His mother decides in a minute: she dries him, runs his
  clothes through the bath, and when the Kikuchis phone at two in the morning, she tells them Haruki was home by
  eleven and the two of them said goodbye at the temple gate. The police search the roads, not the river. Haruki says
  what his mother told him to say, and in time he believes it. He keeps the scarf.
- **At 22 the illness comes.** In his mind Natsumi is alive in Tokyo, and the girl on the posters isn't her. For years
  he writes to her at her parents' address: love, apologies ("I should have held on"), delusions ("they replaced
  you").
- **Last winter** his mother dies in the bath. **This spring** the Kikuchis have Natsumi declared dead and bury an empty
  coffin. Fuyumi reads his letters, which her parents never opened, and decides he knows something.
- **Two weeks ago** she sends him a postcard in her sister's handwriting: "I'm coming home for New Year. Can I visit on
  the 31st? Natsu." On the 27th she leaves Tokyo with a note and without her phone, and her family reports her
  missing. Tonight she plays her sister, the one his letters write to, to make him remember. It's Shutter Island's
  role-play, staged by one grieving woman.
- **Two things are true at once:** Haruki is ill, and Natsumi has never quite left. Everything strange tonight is his
  illness, except a handful of things that happen even with his head clear.

## How it plays

### Tasks, requests and the hidden clean-up
The to-do list carries the night. The first task is hidden and timed: tidy up before she arrives (about two minutes
from her call; the player isn't told). Take out the rubbish, wash the dishes, wipe the kotatsu, fold the laundry, air
the guest room, put your things away. Whatever's left, she sees and comments on. If the pill organiser is still on the
kotatsu she sees that too, and the evening changes: she's more careful, and his illness comes out sooner. After that
come her requests: tea, soba, the bath, the futon, and later more tea. She asks, waits where she is, and the scene
carries on once it's done.

### Talks: reading her face (L.A. Noire, turned round)
- The intimate moments are close-ups: at the kotatsu, on the balcony, at the door. She speaks; her face (a mocapped
  expression) answers before her words; you answer with an act and a thought.
- Answers come in three kinds. **Trust her** (`[ Greet ]`, `[ Let it go ]`). **Look closer** (`[ Question ]`,
  `[ Look ]`): she reacts, and a tell may show. **Accuse** (`[ Accuse ] You're not Natsu.`): offered only when you hold
  evidence, things found or noticed, as in L.A. Noire.
- **The turn:** she's reading your face too. Every question she asks about the past is part of her interrogation, and
  each time you repeat the story about the temple gate, her face tells you she knows it's a lie.
- **The narrator you can't trust:** Haruki's inner monologue tells you what her face means. Off his medication it
  reads anger into fear and lies into grief ("She's smirking"), which is how paranoia reads faces (research §7.2),
  while the performance itself shows a frightened woman. The player learns to trust what they see over what they're
  told.

### The pills
His medication is in the bathroom cabinet, where he hid it while tidying. He can take it after dinner, after
midnight, and when she wakes at two. Each dose quiets the hallucinations and the paranoia in his inner monologue;
none is required. The game never preaches, but it shows him: with his head clear the night gets quieter, and what's
left is real. At two in the morning the same pill can go into his mouth or into her tea.

### What's tracked
- **Karma** (her trust): honest, kind answers raise it; lies, snooping she catches and cruelty lower it. It decides
  how much she lets him see, whether she tells him who she is, and how each ending plays.
- **Evidence** (flags): the tells noticed (the tea, the mole, her hands, the negi, "Haru-nii", the photo the officer
  shows) and the things found (the postcard, her bag, the wall, the tape, the diary). Three or more unlock
  `[ Accuse ]`.
- **Signs** (flags): what happens even with his head clear (the single "moshi", the wet footprints, the 109th bell,
  the bell at the altar, a second voice in the song).
- **Doses** (a count, 0 to 3).

## The three endings

**1. The intended ending: First Light** (初日の出, *hatsuhinode*). He tells the truth: the ice, the scarf in his hand,
running home, the gate story his mother gave him. He gives Fuyumi the scarf. Her face goes through everything, rage,
grief, then something like relief: "Twelve years. Someone finally said it." She doesn't forgive him, but she stays. At
dawn they watch the year's first sunrise from the balcony, over the river where her sister is. His pill organiser is
on the kotatsu where she can see it. At seven the doorbell rings, and he opens the door to Officer Sasaki himself.
*He was the villain of the past: his silence kept her family waiting twelve years.*
- How: the truth told at the end (`[ Remember ]`), and her cup never touched.

**2. The other way it could have gone: Kamikakushi** (神隠し, *spirited away*). He never lets himself see her.
Untreated, afraid, sure something is wearing Natsumi's face (it is, just not the way he thinks), he keeps to the gate
story, and when she goes for the door he stops her. Cut to black on her face; the 109th bell. Morning: the apartment
is spotless, and he washes two cups. The doorbell: Officer Sasaki. "A neighbour saw a young woman come up here last
night." "She left in the night." (The gate story again.) In the corridor a new poster goes up beside the faded one: *Fuyumi Kikuchi, 28, missing since
31 December.* His inner monologue: "Natsumi's coming next year. I should keep the place clean." *He was the villain,
then and now.* Nothing is shown.
- How: the pill in her cup, or `[ Stop her ]` at the door. (A `[ Deny ]` at the end isn't final: she goes for the
  door, and he chooses between calling her back with the truth and stopping her.)

**3. The secret ending: Shiki** (四季 / 死期). Everything in First Light, and more. With his head clear and his mother's
diary open between them, Fuyumi reads what really happened: he went out onto the ice for Natsumi, his hands bled from
it, and his mother lied. Then the phone rings. "Moshi." He answers "moshi moshi", and a girl's voice gives the call
they used in hide-and-seek: "Mō ii yo" ("ready: come and find me"). Fuyumi hears it too, the first time anyone has
heard what he hears. The balcony door is open, and small wet footprints lead to the railing. In the first light, at
the bridge's pillar, there's something red in the ice: Natsumi's mitten. For a moment, in the glass behind Fuyumi, her
sister stands there, eighteen and smiling. Summer and winter in the same room. *He wasn't the villain; the illness and
the lie were real, and so is she.*
- How: everything First Light needs, plus at least two doses, the 00:13 call answered "moshi moshi", and the diary
  read (the wet footprints lead to the drawer).

## The lines

Her lines are short: a beat each, about twelve English words, one take, plain English that will translate cleanly.
Childhood lives in the details, not in speeches. Some of hers:

- "Haru-kun. You got tall."
- "Can I have some tea? My hands are frozen."
- "You cleaned. For me?"
- "Perfect. Just how I like it."
- "Do you still go down to the river?"
- "Mirrors. You've only ever seen me in your head."
- "Mom used to say summer and winter can't be in the same room."
- "Happy New Year, Haru-nii."
- "What happened on the ice?"
- "My name is Fuyumi. I knew you'd open the door for her."

His are acts and thoughts:

- `[ Greet ] She's probably cold. I should let her in.` / `[ Question ] Did she always look like this?`
- `[ Two sugars ] She always took two.` / `[ Ask ] Maybe she takes it differently now.`
- `[ Question ] Wasn't her mole on the other side?` / `[ Let it go ] Twelve years. I'm remembering it wrong.`

- `[ Accuse ] You're not Natsu.`

## Faces (MiniMax post prompts, 4 seconds)

About fifty faces for the talks, in the writers' room's form: what the face visibly does, no words. For example:

- *the sugar*: she sips, her mouth tightens for a moment, then she smiles too widely at the viewer.
- *the mole*: her fingers drift up to the mole under her right eye, then she lowers her hand and looks away.
- *the door*: her eyes flick past the viewer to the door, come back, and she smiles.
- *Haru-nii*: she freezes, her lips part, her eyes fill and she blinks it away, then holds the viewer's gaze.
- *the salt*: she laughs with her whole face, then catches herself and covers her mouth.
- *the gate*: her face goes still and hard; she breathes out slowly through her nose and looks down.

## Care notes

- The illness is Haruki's lived experience, not the monster. The intended and secret endings show him helped; the bad
  ending is a tragedy, never a reveal that "the ill man was the killer". What he did at eighteen, he did before he was
  ill (research §7.5).
- A content note at the start (mental illness, death, implied violence) and a short resources screen at the end.
- The drowning is private and fictional, and so is the town; real cases give us shapes only.
