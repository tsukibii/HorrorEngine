## The pitch

16 August, the last day of Obon, in a river town in Iwate. Haruki Onodera, 25, hasn't let anyone into his late
mother's apartment in months. It's her first Obon since she died, and twelve years to the day since Natsumi Kikuchi,
the girl he loved when they were thirteen, vanished on the night of the river festival. A summer card came in July:
Natsu is coming home for Obon, and she wants to visit. He has twenty minutes to tidy up.

She arrives in a summer storm, soaked, laughing, with a watermelon under her arm. She's everything he remembers:
Natsumi's lilac eyes, Natsumi's laugh, a look that says she's missed him too. They make tea, eat somen, play cards like
they used to, and it's the evening he's waited twelve years for. He just can't remember why it's taken twelve years.
Nor why the officer at the door says another name, nor whose mother is on the phone.

## What kind of game

- First person, one night, one apartment (a 2LDK), its balcony and the building's little garden. About 80 minutes on a
  first playthrough: her lines type out with a blip and can't be skipped.
- **A real visit.** Most of the night is the small things a visit is made of (a towel, tea, somen, a watermelon,
  cards, a cigarette downstairs, ramen at midnight, a futon), each a Chilla's Art-style task: pick up, open, pour, cut,
  carry. Each small thing can stir a memory he can't quite reach. It should feel like being there, with amnesia, in an
  evening ordinary enough to believe.
- L.A. Noire's close-ups, but **the face you're reading may not be hers**: the camera is Haruki's eye.
- **The story runs one way.** Dialogue choices change what she says back and what you learn, and open secrets. What
  decides the ending is mostly the pills.
- **A card game** with standard cards, *shinkei-suijaku* (Concentration: "nervous breakdown" in Japanese), its options
  and results following the way the night is going.
- **Three endings, three pieces of the truth**, each a cutscene slot outlined here and made in the director, then an
  ending title card. Only all three together show what happened.
- No chases, no quick-time events, no game overs. English text first, simple enough to translate later. No voices.

## The place and the day

- **Kirisawa** (fictional), a small river town in Iwate, Tohoku. Below the bridge, a low weir of stones crosses the
  river; kids used to run across it to watch the fireworks from the sandbank. It was forbidden.
- **16 August 2026**, the last day of Obon, when families send the dead back with a fire at the door (*okuribi*). The
  festival's fireworks are heard and seen as flashes from the balcony. From 17:30, in the heat and the cicadas, to
  first light.
- **The weather:** an evening storm (*yūdachi*) breaks as she arrives and comes back in the small hours to take the
  power.
- **The apartment**: unit 302, third floor of a 1990s block, thin walls, and the building's **little garden** at the
  foot of the stairs: a bench, a few potted plants, the bicycle shed, a vending machine's glow.

## What the pills decide

The camera is Haruki's eye, and the evening begins the way he sees it: he's been off his pills for two weeks. **His
illness rewrites her and only her.** Everything else stays true: her card, the officer, her mother on the phone, the
answering machine, the news, her bag. That's where the player finds the truth while he can't.

| | Off his pills (how the night starts) | After a pill |
|---|---|---|
| **What he calls her** | Natsu (a variable in every line he speaks or thinks) | Natsuki, Ko-natsu |
| **Her eyes** | Natsumi's lilac | her own brown |
| **Her faces** | the romance he's living: shy smiles, a blush, a look held a beat too long, her hand finding his | what's really there: glad, then puzzled, polite, uneasy, frightened |
| **Her corrections** | never seen: when she says "Haru-nii, I'm Natsuki", the line simply isn't there | on screen, gently at first, then plainly |
| **Some of her lines** | warmer than she meant them ("I missed you." for "I missed everyone.") | as she said them |
| **His head** | the altar's bell, the TV saying his name, figures on the balcony, knocking in the oshiire | quiet |
| **The cards** | his memory lies (a card he's sure of isn't there), and she lets him win | the cards are what they are, and she wins |

So about a dozen key moments are shot twice in mocap: **his version** (the romance) and **hers** (what's really
happening). The player only ever sees hers after a pill, or in the dark ending's flashes. The pills are tasks on the
to-do list three times (after dinner, at midnight, at two), each with a time to do it in; let it pass and it fails,
struck out in red. The first one doesn't cure him (no pill works in minutes); it's the moment he chooses to stop
looking away. Game logic, not pharmacology.

## The card game: shinkei-suijaku (Concentration)

"We always played this at your mom's." His mother's old deck, a few cards face down on the low table, pairs turned
two at a time; most pairs wins. Its Japanese name means *nervous breakdown*, and it's a game of memory. The stake, as
when they were kids: **the loser answers one question.** It's a new rules module for the board games
(`Content/Games/TheVisitor/BoardGames/shinkei_suijaku.js`, a deck from `_lib.js`), with settings the night sets:

| | Off his pills | After a pill |
|---|---|---|
| **The cards** | his memory lies: now and then a card he's sure of turns out to be another ("I was sure it was there.") | true |
| **Who wins** | she lets him (his version: she laughs, delighted to lose) | she does, easily: Ko-natsu always won. Natsumi was hopeless at it. |
| **If he wins, he asks** | romance questions: `[ Ask ] Did you miss me?` | real ones: `[ Ask ] Why did you really come?` |
| **If she wins, she asks** | "Where were you at eleven that night?" `[ Home ] Mom said so.` | the same, and more answers open: `[ Don't know ] I don't remember coming home.` |

What the answers turn up are flags, the pieces the endings use.

## Two mysteries: her, and you

### Clues about her
What she knows (all the childhood, the festival, the fireflies, the time he broke his arm: she was there, smaller,
behind them) and what she doesn't (the private things between Natsumi and him); she calls him Haru-nii, where Natsu
said Haru-kun; she eats right-handed; she pushes her negi aside; she smokes; she wins at cards; the age she looks; her
student ID; the officer's "Natsuki-chan"; her mother on the phone.

### Clues about you
The player starts out investigating her, and slowly notices the evidence is about him.
- **The answers you're offered tell on you.** Some choices only someone in his state would think of: `[ Lock it ]
  Twice. Then check it again.`, `[ Turn off the TV ] Before it says my name.`, `[ Hide my hands ] She doesn't need to
  see them.`
- **Her questions are probes, and the true answers expose him.** "Do you still go down to the river?" `[ Lie ] I
  don't go out.` / `[ Truth ] Every year. Today.`
- **His own thoughts correct themselves.** *"I walked her home that night."* backs up and becomes *"I walked home
  that night."*
- **What your hands show.** His palms are scarred (from the weir's stones) whenever he reaches for something; the
  inner monologue never mentions it.
- **What you find in your own home.** The card signed *Natsuki*. Notes on the fridge in his handwriting ("Don't answer
  the phone after eleven"; "Pills: did you?"). The taped closet. And two cucumber horses on his mother's Obon altar:
  one for his mother, one he made for Natsumi. Part of him has always known she's dead.
- **The memory flickers.** Each small moment almost gives him back something; the player collects the fragments.

## What the player believes, in order (the believability ladder)

1. Natsu has come back, and she still likes him. → Twelve years; they were children; it's Obon, a night for
   homecomings.
2. A black-bordered letter in his unopened mail: Natsumi was declared dead this spring, and the Kikuchis invite him to
   her first Obon. → A mistake? Then who's sitting at his table? *(a ghost come home for Obon)*
3. She looks younger than he expected (in his head Natsumi grew up to 25 beside him; the woman at the door is 21).
   She says Haru-nii; Natsu always said Haru-kun. She eats right-handed; she smokes. → Teasing; Tokyo; he's
   remembering wrong.
4. A woman from the next town has been missing since the 12th; the news, then the officer, ask about her. The officer greets her as "Natsuki-chan" and calls Haruki "the Onodera boy. From back then. Twelve
   years today." → He misspoke; everyone was questioned back then. → What happened back then? *(the first time the
   player suspects Haruki)*
5. The phone: "Is my daughter there? Natsuki?" → Wrong number. → Something is wearing Natsu's face *(possession)*.
6. His scarred hands, his notes, the second cucumber horse, the pills, the nurse's messages, the wall in his closet,
   the cards that won't stay where he saw them. → He's ill, and the romance is in his head *(the Shutter Island
   reading, and Joker's)*.
7. After a pill: "Haru-nii... I'm Natsuki." She has been all night *(the truth about her)*.
8. What happened at the weir: never told outright. Each ending gives a piece.

## The truth, and how it's split

**What happened (for us):** on 16 August 2014 Natsumi told Haruki, on the sandbank after the fireworks, that her
family was moving to Tokyo at the end of the summer. He told her he liked her. Walking home, the strap of her geta
snapped (a bad omen); she left the sandal on the bank and ran barefoot across the weir, the river high from the
afternoon's storm. She slipped. He caught her wrist and held it, the current pulling, and then he let go. Whether he
couldn't hold on or didn't, he has never let himself know. He ran home soaked, his palms torn by the weir. His mother
cleaned his hands, washed his clothes, and when the Kikuchis phoned at two in the morning, told them Haruki had been
home by eleven. He has lived inside that sentence ever since. In July, Natsuki found his letters to her sister ("I
should have held on") and wrote to him.

**No one confesses.** The player is finding out with him, and he doesn't remember. The pieces:

| Ending | The piece it shows |
|---|---|
| **Okuribi** (intended) | **Her side.** Natsumi was leaving that summer, and she loved him back (her Tanabata wish from that July, kept by her family: "I wish Haru-kun would wait for me"). And the Kikuchis never believed he was home by eleven |
| **Thank You for Tonight** (the other way) | **His guilt.** A page of his diary: twelve years of living a lie he can't name ("Mom said I was home. I let everyone believe it."). Feelings, never the cause |
| **Don't Let Go** (secret, dark) | **The night itself,** in flashes: the weir, her wrist in his hand, his fingers opening. Then the present, repeating it the other way |

## The three endings

Each ending is **a cutscene slot**: a rough outline here, made in full in the director, where the whole last part can
be changed freely. After each comes **an ending title card**: the ending's name and a line, with room for more (what
it means, what's still hidden, a nudge towards the others).

### 1. The intended ending: Okuribi (送り火, *the sending-off fire*)
- **How:** at least two pill tasks done.
- **The cutscene (rough):** first light; he calls her Ko-natsu; they talk about Natsumi, not about what he did (he
  doesn't know); she gives him Natsumi's Tanabata wish from that summer ("I wish Haru-kun would wait for me"); "Your
  mother said you were home by eleven. Mom never believed it."; they light the sending-off fire at the door for
  Natsumi and his mother; she goes home, her eyes brown in the firelight.
- **The title card:** *Okuribi.* (The piece: her side.)

### 2. The other way it could have gone: Thank You for Tonight
- **How:** anything else (fewer than two pills, without the dark ending's conditions).
- **The cutscene (rough):** the romance never breaks; breakfast; she leaves, bowing at the door; the next day the
  answering machine blinks ("Thank you for last night, Haru-nii. It felt like when we were kids. ...You called me
  Natsu all night. I didn't mind."); he writes in his diary, and we read the page over his shoulder: twelve years of a
  lie he can't name, nothing about what it was.
- **The title card:** *Thank You for Tonight.* (The piece: his guilt.)

### 3. The secret, dark ending: Don't Let Go
- **How:** never a pill (every task failed), the romance leaned into at every turn, and her room entered at two
  (`[ Watch her sleep ]`).
- **The cutscene (rough):** the clock skips from 02:10 to 03:40; he's at the low table in the dark, calm, his palms
  wet; a knock: "Police. A neighbour reported a disturbance."; the younger officer goes down the hall to the guest
  room and stops at the door (we see only his face); a revolver, drawn: "On your knees! Hands where I can see them!";
  kneeling on the tatami, the inner monologue all wrong ("You'll wake her." "She said she'd stay."); between its
  lines, the night replayed in flashes with her real faces (the confusion, the fear, the corrections he never saw),
  then the weir: a girl's wrist in his hand, his fingers opening; the last line, "This time I held on." Nothing of the
  violence is shown.
- **The title card:** *Don't Let Go.* (The piece: the night itself.)

## The lines

Short: a beat each, about twelve English words, plain English that will translate cleanly. Childhood in the details
(the river, the fireflies, the sandbank, ramune bottles, a little sister always behind them). Hers, as she says them:

- "Haru-nii. You got tall."
- "I brought a watermelon. It's half the reason I'm soaked."
- "I'm freezing. Isn't that silly, in August?"
- "Who's the second one for, Haru-nii?"
- "We always played this at your mom's. Loser answers a question."
- "You two never let me come to the sandbank."
- "Don't tell my mom I smoke."
- "Haru-nii... I'm Natsuki."
- "I'm starving. Do you have anything?"

The same moment, his version and hers (off the pills the player sees the first; after a pill, the second):

- *at the door*: she smiles as if she's waited years for this / her smile falters when he says "Natsu".
- *the cards*: she groans, delighted, as he wins / she turns the last pair without looking.
- *the garden*: she leans close and offers him the cigarette / she's been crying, and keeps the bench between them.

His answers (an act and a thought; {her} is Natsu or Natsuki, by the pills):

- `[ Greet ] She's probably cold. I should let her inside.` / `[ Question ] She looks younger than I remember.`
- `[ Two sugars ] {her} always took two.` / `[ Ask ] Maybe she takes it differently now.`
- `[ Hold her hand ] She wants me to.` / `[ Stay where you are ] Not yet.`
- `[ Lock it ] Twice. Then check it again.` / `[ Hide my hands ] She doesn't need to see them.`

## Faces (MiniMax post prompts, 4 seconds)

About sixty: her real faces for the whole night, and a second, romantic set for the dozen moments his version
rewrites. For example:

- *his version, the door*: she beams, her eyes shining, and looks down shyly, then back at the viewer.
- *hers, the door*: her smile falters, her brows draw together for a moment, then she smiles again, unsure.
- *his version, the garden*: she smiles sideways at the viewer through the smoke and holds out the cigarette.
- *hers, the garden*: she wipes her eyes with the back of her wrist, breathes out smoke, and won't look at the viewer.
- *hers, "I'm Natsuki"*: her chin trembles, her eyes fill, and she looks at the viewer as if at a stranger.
