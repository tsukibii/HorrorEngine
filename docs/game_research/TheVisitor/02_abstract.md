## The pitch

16 August, the last day of Obon, in a river town in Iwate. Haruki Onodera, 28, hasn't let anyone into his late
mother's apartment in months. It's her first Obon since she died, and twelve years to the day since Natsumi Kikuchi,
the girl he loved when they were sixteen, vanished on the night of the river festival. A summer card came in July:
Natsu is coming home for Obon, and she wants to visit. He has twenty minutes to tidy up.

She arrives in a summer storm, soaked, laughing, with a watermelon under her arm. She's everything he remembers:
Natsumi's lilac eyes, Natsumi's laugh, and now, at last, a look that says she feels the same. They make tea, eat
somen, play cards like they used to, and it's the evening he's waited twelve years for. He just can't remember why
it's taken twelve years. Nor why the officer at the door says another name, nor whose mother is on the phone, nor
whose boyfriend.

## What kind of game

- First person, one night, one apartment (a 2LDK), its balcony and the building's little garden. About 80 minutes on a
  first playthrough: her lines type out with a blip and can't be skipped.
- **A real visit.** Most of the night is the small things a visit is made of (a towel, tea, somen, a watermelon,
  cards, a cigarette downstairs, ramen at midnight, a futon), each a Chilla's Art-style task: pick up, open, pour, cut,
  carry. Each small thing can stir a memory he can't quite reach. It should feel like being there, with amnesia, in an
  evening ordinary enough to believe.
- L.A. Noire's close-ups, but **the face you're reading may not be hers**: the camera is Haruki's eye.
- **The story runs one way.** Dialogue choices change what she says back and what you learn, and open secrets. What
  decides the ending is mostly the pills.
- **A card game** with standard cards, *shinkei-suijaku* (Concentration: "nervous breakdown" in Japanese), its options
  and results following the way the night is going.
- **Three endings, three pieces of the truth**, each a cutscene slot outlined here and made in the director, then an
  ending title card. Only all three together show what happened, and who he is.
- No chases, no quick-time events, no game overs. English text first, simple enough to translate later. No voices.

## How it's written

- **The player has amnesia, and so does Haruki's head.** His thoughts know only what's in front of him and what his
  body remembers ("My hands know where the cups are." "Two. My hand already knows."). He never states the backstory,
  her name's history, or what happened.
- **Facts come from outside him, once each:** the to-do list ("Clean up the apartment before Natsu arrives"), her
  lines ("Twelve years, Haru-nii. Can you believe it?"), the props (the card, the photo, the pharmacy bag), the officer,
  the phone. Each is a small surprise the player and Haruki take in together.
- **He never speaks a line.** His answers are a small human reaction to what just happened, an act and a thought:
  `[ Greet ] She looks cold, welcome her inside --- [ Question ] She looks different.` The player imagines what he
  said.
- **The gaps are the horror.** "Twelve years since what?" "Why two?" "Lie about what?" "Why do I see trees?" Each
  thought ends on a question the night answers later, or never.
- **Pseudo-code on the chart**: only literal things (who does what with which thing, short lines, sounds as cues),
  never prose (docs/LESSONS.md).

## The place and the day

- **Kirisawa** (fictional), a small river town in Iwate, Tohoku. On the far bank, the shrine woods: a path along the
  top of a steep bank, where teenagers went after the fireworks. Below it the river runs fast after rain.
- **16 August 2026**, the last day of Obon, when families send the dead back with a fire at the door (*okuribi*). The
  festival's fireworks are heard and seen as flashes from the balcony. From 17:30, in the heat and the cicadas, to
  first light.
- **The weather:** an evening storm (*yūdachi*) breaks as she arrives and comes back in the small hours to take the
  power. Twelve years ago it rained the same way that afternoon.
- **The apartment**: unit 302, third floor of a 1990s block, thin walls, and the building's **little garden** at the
  foot of the stairs: a bench, a few potted plants, the bicycle shed, a vending machine's glow.

**The names.** Natsumi (夏美) and Natsuki (夏希): both "summer", one letter apart in English and one kana apart in

**The echo.** Both sisters see him as a big brother; one called him Haru-kun, the other still calls him Haru-nii
("big brother Haru"). Both, at the age they are, have someone else. The night repeats the summer, and the player
won't see it until the end.

## What the pills decide

The camera is Haruki's eye, and the evening begins the way he sees it: he's been off his pills for two weeks. **His
illness rewrites her and only her.** Everything else stays true: her card, the officer, her mother and her boyfriend
on the phone, the answering machine, the news, her bag. That's where the player finds the truth while he can't.

| | Off his pills (how the night starts) | After a pill |
|---|---|---|
| **What he calls her** | Natsu (a variable in every line he speaks or thinks) | Natsuki, Ko-natsu |
| **Her eyes** | Natsumi's lilac | her own brown |
| **Her faces** | the romance he's living: shy smiles, a blush, a look held a beat too long, her hand finding his | what's really there: glad, then puzzled, polite, uneasy, frightened |
| **Her corrections** | never seen: when she says "Haru-nii, I'm Natsuki", the line simply isn't there | on screen, gently at first, then plainly |
| **Her boyfriend** | never mentioned: her lines about Ren are left out | there, often: Ren says she shouldn't have come |
| **Some of her lines** | warmer than she meant them ("I missed you." for "I missed everyone.") | as she said them |
| **His head** | the altar's bell, the TV saying his name, figures on the balcony, knocking in the oshiire | quiet |
| **The cards** | his memory lies (a card he's sure of isn't there), and she lets him win | the cards are what they are, and she wins |

So about a dozen key moments are shot twice in mocap: **his version** (the romance) and **hers** (what's really
happening). The player only ever sees hers after a pill, or in the dark ending's flashes. The pills are tasks on the
to-do list three times (after dinner, at midnight, at two), each with a time to do it in; let it pass and it fails,
struck out in red. The first one doesn't cure him (no pill works in minutes); it's the moment he chooses to stop
looking away. Game logic, not pharmacology.

## The card game: shinkei-suijaku (Concentration)

"We always played this at your mom's." His mother's old deck, a few cards face down on the low table, pairs turned
two at a time; most pairs wins. Its Japanese name means *nervous breakdown*, and it's a game of memory. The stake, as
when they were kids: **the loser answers one question.** It's a new rules module for the board games
(`Content/Games/TheVisitor/BoardGames/shinkei_suijaku.js`, a deck from `_lib.js`), with settings the night sets:

| | Off his pills | After a pill |
|---|---|---|
| **The cards** | his memory lies: now and then a card he's sure of turns out to be another ("I was sure it was there.") | true |
| **Who wins** | she lets him (his version: she laughs, delighted to lose) | she does, easily: Ko-natsu always won. Natsumi was hopeless at it. |
| **If he wins, he asks** | romance questions: `[ Ask ] Did you ever think about us?` | real ones: `[ Ask ] Why did you really come?` |
| **If she wins, she asks** | "Where were you at eleven that night?" `[ Home ] Mom said so.` | the same, and more answers open: `[ Don't know ] I don't remember coming home.` / `[ The woods ] ...I followed them.` |

What the answers turn up are flags, the pieces the endings use.

## Two mysteries: her, and you

### Clues about her
What she knows (all the childhood, the festival, the fireflies, the time he broke his arm: she was there, smaller,
behind them) and what she doesn't (the private things between Natsumi and him); she calls him Haru-nii, where Natsu
said Haru-kun; she eats right-handed; she pushes her negi aside; she smokes; she wins at cards; the age she looks; her
student ID; the officer's "Natsuki-chan"; her mother on the phone; her boyfriend on the phone.

### Clues about you
The player starts out investigating her, and slowly notices the evidence is about him.
- **The answers you're offered tell on you.** Some choices only someone in his state would think of: `[ Lock it ]
  Twice. Then check it again.`, `[ Turn off the TV ] Before it says my name.`, `[ Hide my hands ] She doesn't need to
  see them.` And some show a temper he doesn't admit to: when the officer mentions the Sakamoto boy, `[ Say it ] He
  was no good for her.`
- **Her questions are probes, and the true answers expose him.** "Do you still go down to the river?" `[ Lie ] I
  don't go out.` / `[ Truth ] Every year. Today.`
- **His own thoughts correct themselves.** *"I walked home with her that night."* backs up and becomes *"I walked home
  that night."*
- **What your hands show.** His palms are scarred (from the rocks of the riverbank) whenever he reaches for something;
  the inner monologue never mentions it.
- **What you find in your own home.** The card signed *Natsuki*. Notes on the fridge in his handwriting ("Don't answer
  the phone after eleven"; "Pills: did you?"). The taped closet, and on its wall a festival photo of Natsumi with a boy,
  the boy's face scratched out. And two cucumber horses on his mother's Obon altar: one for his mother, one he made for
  Natsumi. Part of him has always known she's dead.
- **The memory flickers.** Each small moment almost gives him back something; the player collects the fragments.

## What the player believes, in order (the believability ladder)

1. Natsu has come back, and this time she feels the same. → Twelve years; they were kids; it's Obon, a night for
   homecomings.
2. A black-bordered letter in his unopened mail: Natsumi was declared dead this spring, and the Kikuchis invite him to
   her first Obon. → A mistake? Then who's sitting at his table? *(a ghost come home for Obon)*
3. She looks younger than he expected (in his head Natsumi grew up to 28 beside him; the woman at the door is 23).
   She says Haru-nii; Natsu always said Haru-kun. She eats right-handed; she smokes. → Teasing; Tokyo; he's
   remembering wrong.
4. A woman from the next town has been missing since the 12th; the news, then the officer, ask about her. The officer
   greets the visitor as "Natsuki-chan", calls Haruki "the Onodera boy. From back then. Twelve years today.", and
   wonders aloud whether the Sakamoto boy ever told everything he knew. → He misspoke; everyone was questioned back
   then; it was the boyfriend. → What happened back then? *(the first time the player suspects anyone)*
5. The phone: "Is my daughter there? Natsuki?" → Wrong number. → Something is wearing Natsu's face *(possession)*.
6. His scarred hands, his notes, the scratched-out boy, the second cucumber horse, the pills, the nurse's messages,
   the cards that won't stay where he saw them. → He's ill, and the romance is in his head *(the Shutter Island
   reading, and Joker's)*.
7. After a pill: "Haru-nii... I'm Natsuki." She has been all night *(the truth about her)*.
8. 01:30, the phone: "Is Natsuki there? It's Ren." Someone else, again *(the echo)*.
9. What happened in the woods: never told outright. Each ending gives a piece.

## The truth, and how it's split

**What happened (for us):** that summer, Natsumi, sixteen, started seeing Shun Sakamoto, a boy from the year above.
Haruki found out at the festival, when he saw them holding hands under the fireworks. Afterwards he followed them to
the shrine woods, and when Shun had gone home he caught up with her on the path above the river. He told her he loved
her. She was kind about it, which was worse: "Haru-kun, you're like my brother." It became a fight: about Shun, about
her not seeing him, about everything a sixteen-year-old says when he can't stop. She tried to walk away; he grabbed
her shoulders and shoved her, once, hard. Her geta slipped on the wet path, and she went down the bank into the river,
high from the afternoon's storm, and it took her. He scrambled down after her, tore his hands on the rocks, and she
was gone. He never meant to kill her. He is the reason she died.

He ran home soaked, carrying her geta. His mother cleaned his hands, washed his clothes, and when the Kikuchis phoned
at two in the morning, told them Haruki had been home by eleven. The town looked at Shun, the last one seen with her,
and kept looking for years. Haruki has lived inside his mother's sentence ever since; the illness grew round it. In
July, Natsuki found his letters to her sister ("I'm sorry. I'd take it back.") and wrote to him.

**No one confesses.** The player is finding out with him, and he doesn't remember. The pieces:

| Ending | The piece it shows |
|---|---|
| **Okuribi** (intended) | **Her side.** Natsumi loved him, as a brother (her Tanabata wish from that July, kept by her family: "I wish Haru-kun will always be my big brother"). And: "Shun always said you followed us into the woods that night. Nobody believed him. Your mother said you were home." |
| **Thank You for Tonight** (the other way) | **His guilt.** A page of his diary: twelve years of a lie he can't name, and a jealousy that never died ("Mom said I was home. I let them blame him. I'm glad they did."). Feelings, never the cause |
| **Stay** (secret, dark) | **The night itself,** in flashes: the fireworks, two hands held, the path in the woods, "you're like my brother", his hands on her shoulders, the bank, the river. Then the present, repeating it |

Only all three together answer the question the game asks: she saw him as a brother and loved someone else; he
followed them; he pushed her; his mother covered it and let another boy carry it; and he lived the lie until it came
back for her sister, who also calls him brother, and also has someone else.

## The three endings

Each ending is **a cutscene slot**: a rough outline here, made in full in the director, where the whole last part can
be changed freely. After each comes **an ending title card**: the ending's name and a line, with room for more (what
it means, what's still hidden, a nudge towards the others).

### 1. The intended ending: Okuribi (送り火, *the sending-off fire*)
- **How:** at least two pill tasks done.
- **The cutscene (rough):** first light; he calls her Ko-natsu; they talk about Natsumi, not about what he did (he
  doesn't know); she gives him Natsumi's Tanabata wish from that July ("I wish Haru-kun will always be my big
  brother"); "Shun always said you followed us into the woods that night. Nobody believed him. Your mother said you
  were home."; he has no answer, and the player has none either; they light the sending-off fire at the door for
  Natsumi and his mother; she catches the first train, her eyes brown in the firelight.
- **The title card:** *Okuribi.* (The piece: her side.)

### 2. The other way it could have gone: Thank You for Tonight
- **How:** anything else (fewer than two pills, without the dark ending's conditions).
- **The cutscene (rough):** the romance never breaks; breakfast; she leaves for the first train, bowing at the door;
  the next day the answering machine blinks ("Thank you for last night, Haru-nii. It felt like when we were kids.
  ...You called me Natsu all night. I didn't mind."); he writes in his diary, and we read the page over his shoulder:
  twelve years of a lie he can't name, a boy he still hates, nothing about what happened.
- **The title card:** *Thank You for Tonight.* (The piece: his guilt.)

### 3. The secret, dark ending: Stay
- **How:** never a pill (every task failed), the romance leaned into at every turn, Ren's call lied about, and her room
  entered at two (`[ Watch her sleep ]`).
- **The cutscene (rough):** the clock skips from 02:10 to 03:40; he's at the low table in the dark, calm, his palms
  wet; a knock: "Police. A neighbour reported a disturbance."; the younger officer goes down the hall to the guest
  room and stops at the door (we see only his face); a revolver, drawn: "On your knees! Hands where I can see them!";
  kneeling on the tatami, the inner monologue all wrong ("You'll wake her." "She said she'd stay."); between its
  lines, the night replayed in flashes with her real faces (the confusion, the fear, the corrections he never saw,
  Ren's name), then the summer twelve years ago: two hands held under the fireworks, the path in the woods, "you're
  like my brother", his hands on her shoulders, the bank, the river; the last line, "This time, she stayed." Nothing
  of the violence is shown.
- **The title card:** *Stay.* (The piece: the night itself.)

## The lines

Short: a beat each, about twelve English words, plain English that will translate cleanly. Childhood in the details
(the river, the fireflies, the sandbank, ramune bottles, a little sister always behind them). Hers, as she says them:

- "Haru-nii. You got tall."
- "I brought a watermelon. It's half the reason I'm soaked."
- "I'm freezing. Isn't that silly, in August?"
- "Who's the second one for, Haru-nii?"
- "We always played this at your mom's. Loser answers a question."
- "You two never let me come to the woods."
- "Don't tell my mom I smoke."
- "Ren says I shouldn't have come."
- "Haru-nii... I'm Natsuki."
- "I'm starving. Do you have anything?"

The same moment, his version and hers (off the pills the player sees the first; after a pill, the second):

- *at the door*: she smiles as if she's waited years for this / her smile falters when he says "Natsu".
- *the cards*: she groans, delighted, as he wins / she turns the last pair without looking.
- *the garden*: she leans close and offers him the cigarette / she's been crying, and keeps the bench between them.

His answers are an act and a thought, and they only know what's in front of him (see How it's written):

- `[ Greet ] She looks cold, welcome her inside --- [ Question ] She looks different.`
- `[ Smile ] She knows me. Smile back --- [ Look ] Twelve years? It feels like last week.`
- `[ Mom ] They're both for Mom. Aren't they? --- [ Say nothing ] I don't remember making two.`
- `[ Tense ] That name. My jaw is tight --- [ Blank ] Sakamoto? I don't know him.`
- `[ Home ] Home. Mom always said so --- [ What night ] What night? --- [ Trees ] Trees. Rain. Why do I see trees?`
- `[ Hold her hand ] She wants me to --- [ Wait ] What night? Everyone keeps saying that night.`

## Faces (MiniMax post prompts, 4 seconds)

About sixty: her real faces for the whole night, and a second, romantic set for the dozen moments his version
rewrites. For example:

- *his version, the door*: she beams, her eyes shining, and looks down shyly, then back at the viewer.
- *hers, the door*: her smile falters, her brows draw together for a moment, then she smiles again, unsure.
- *his version, the garden*: she smiles sideways at the viewer through the smoke and holds out the cigarette.
- *hers, the garden*: she wipes her eyes with the back of her wrist, breathes out smoke, and won't look at the viewer.
- *hers, "he was no good for her"*: she goes still, her eyes narrow slightly, and she studies the viewer's face.
- *hers, "I'm Natsuki"*: her chin trembles, her eyes fill, and she looks at the viewer as if at a stranger.

## Care notes

- **He is the reason she died, and he didn't mean it.** What happened in the woods was a sixteen-year-old's fight that
  spiralled, a shove and a fall, years before he was ill. The illness isn't the cause; it grew round the lie. The
  game never excuses the shove, and never makes his illness the villain.
- The dark ending is a tragedy of untreated illness, jealousy and a buried lie repeating, staged in fragments, never a
  "gotcha" (research §7.5). The intended ending is the one where he sees her.
- The romance he imagines stays tender and imagined (smiles, a hand, a cigarette offered): never sexual, and never
  something she did.
- A content note at the start (mental illness, the death of a teenager, drowning, implied violence) and a short
  resources screen at the end.
