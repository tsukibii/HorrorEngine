# References: the shapes our games borrow

What the script generator's bible and cards draw on, and what Claude reviews a draft against. These are structures
and techniques described in our own words from general knowledge of the games and forms named, not their content.
Use them as shapes, never as material.

## 1. The anthology episode (the default shape for a 15 to 20 minute game)

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 5% | A strange image or sound before anything is explained. Over before the player is sure what they saw. |
| The ordinary | 15% | A normal place, a normal reason to be there, someone glad to see you. Warmth the story will spend later. |
| The first wrongness | 10% | One small thing out of place: a line said twice, a photo that changed, a smell. The first choice. |
| The tightening | 30% | Tasks between scenes; each scene a little worse; choices that cost something. A scare every few minutes, then quiet. |
| The turn | 15% | What's really going on is shown, not told: the player works it out a beat before anyone says it. |
| The last choices | 15% | Answers that decide the ending (karma); the character reacts to who the player has been. |
| The coda | 10% | The ending: a final image and a line. The bad one may cost the hero their life; it plays as an ending, not a game over. |

## 2. Resident Evil 7: first person, one house, a family (structure)

- **The point of view is the player's eye**, low and close: people come right up to you; you can't look away
  for long. Ours is the same lens (DIRECTING.md §1).
- **One place, learnt room by room:** a house that opens up as you go (locked doors, keys, shortcuts), so dread
  builds from knowing exactly where the danger lives.
- **Set pieces between the exploring:** a dinner where you're held at the table, a chase through rooms you know,
  then quiet again. Our scenes and timed tasks are that rhythm.
- **Found footage and notes** fill in the past without stopping the present; a short playable flashback can
  change how a room reads.
- **A family as the horror:** people who were ordinary once, and still talk as if they are.

## 3. Five Hearts Under One Roof: branching and simple acting (structure)

- **Live-action acting scenes with choices between them:** the acting is mostly people standing and talking to
  you, framed close; the choices are short replies.
- **Relationships carry the branching:** how you treat each person changes their scenes and their ending; extra
  scenes unlock from what you chose, and epilogues close each route.
- **Simple scenes, many of them:** a scene is a few lines, a reaction, a choice. That's our 80% simple body budget
  and our 7 to 8 second takes.

## 4. Japanese psychological horror: techniques

- **Ma (the pause):** silence held a beat too long; a line left unfinished. Write pauses as their own clips.
- **The ordinary made wrong:** a smile that stays too long, politeness that never breaks, a home kept perfectly.
- **Restraint:** show less (a shape at the end of a hall, a face in a reflection, a voice from the next room).
  The reveal is small and close, not loud.
- **Grief and guilt as the monster:** the haunting is about what the hero did or didn't do; karma makes the player
  answer for it.
- **Sound first:** a sound tells you where to look before anything appears (DIRECTING.md: a cue before every reveal).

## 5. Roblox horror: the mechanics the young players know

The games most played by the Roblox generation (DOORS, Piggy, The Mimic, Evade, Pressure, Apeirophobia) are loved
for simple, readable rules, not hard puzzles. What we take:
- **A warning, then seconds to act:** the lights flicker, a sound grows; hide now (DOORS' Rush). The same entity
  can come back several times (Ambush): leave the hiding place when it fades, hide again when it returns.
- **Don't hide too long:** a hiding place that turns on you if you stay (DOORS' Hide): hiding is a choice, not a
  rest.
- **Rules a child can say in one line:** it's blind, so crouch and be quiet (Figure); look at it to make it leave
  (Screech); don't move while it's looking.
- **Find the items, then escape** while something patrols (Piggy): a short list, ticked one by one.
- **Chapters with save stations** (The Mimic): a death sends you back a little, never far. Our branch map is that.
- **Chases** through rooms you already know (Evade): run to a door you've seen.

Our versions are `play` mechanics (STORY_FORMAT §9.8): `hide`, `run`, `collect`, `sneak`, `hold`, `search`,
`escape`, each with a one-line rule, a warning before, and results that branch.

## 6. Yandere and obsession (the romance turned)

- **Devotion first:** she is sweet, attentive, remembers everything you said; the player should like her.
- **The tells:** she knows things she shouldn't; your phone, your friends, your past come up; a smile that holds.
- **The turn is about you:** jealousy of someone else, fear you'll leave; the same staging that was tender
  (a hand on your cheek, her face close) becomes a threat (the hand on your throat, the door locked behind her).
- **She is never only a monster:** the good ending exists because you can reach her; the bad one because you didn't.

## 7. How a draft is reviewed (Claude, against this page)

1. The shape: does it follow §1's beats and shares for its length? Is there quiet after each scare?
2. The wrongness: is the first one small enough, and does each scene add one thing?
3. The choices: three replies, one silent, each with a different karma; does each one change how the next scene
   plays, even slightly?
4. The deaths: each one is earned (warned, then repeated), names its branch to return to, and the branch map shows what was missed.
5. The lines: one beat each (12 words of English), pauses written as clips, nothing explained that could be shown.
6. The bodies: one plain action per clip at most, simple 80% of the time (docs/KIMODO.md).
7. The ending: earned by the karma, each of the three different in the final image, not only the words.
